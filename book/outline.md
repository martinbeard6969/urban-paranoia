# Urban Paranoia: chapter outline

**Shape:** 30 chapters in three acts, plus a short epilogue. Present-day chapters (spring–autumn 2026) are in **Nell's first person**. Seven **interludes** step back into the Table's history in close third person, each a short, self-contained story that shifts how the reader sees the present. The book opens on **Day 0** (14 Oct 1962) and closes on the 64th anniversary, **Day 23,376** (Wed 14 Oct 2026).

**The spine:** Nell moves from *collecting evidence* (Act 1), to *seeing the shape* (Act 2, ending at the Wall), to *acting with other people* (Act 3). The Table is never defeated. It's **surprised**.

**Drafting priorities:** a sinister, compelling plot; variable pacing; memorable characters; at the standard of a Nebula Award-winning novella (full rules in `CLAUDE.md`, Voice & style).

**Pacing rule:** every chapter knows its speed. **Slow** stretches disclose (history, a document, Crane's context, an interlude). **Fast** stretches thrill: action, less information, short paragraphs, nothing explained until afterwards, if at all. Alternate them across the book and within chapters, and never let two slow chapters run back to back in the present day. **Slow never means dull** (Martin, 28 Oct 2026): every slow stretch carries a live question, a threat or a wonder, and suspense, wonder or thrill is present on every page. Information is easy to understand; its meaning is hard to fathom. Act One's fast beats: the Coyle's keycard (ch 6), Graham's door (ch 7), the night at DC-3 and the bus stop (ch 10).

**Rhythm rule:** whenever the Tenants speak, long observed paragraphs are broken by single centred lines of counsel (as in chapter 1).

**Typography rule:** centred capitals (`>`) are reserved for the Tenants. Letters, documents, stamps and notes use `>>` (left-aligned, ruled). Counsel quoted inside a document is set as a document.

**Paper rule (Act One pacing, 28 Oct 2026):** in Act One only **two documents** appear on the page: the 1971 manifest (ch 2) and the Archivist's letter (ch 6). Everything else Nell finds through people, places, her own skill and her senses: what someone remembers, what she does, what she hears. Logs, screenshots and enclosures are told in a line of her working, never reproduced. The ARG keeps its scans and puzzles, because the blog is where Nell posts her paper (see `bible/open-questions.md`, Settled).

**Cobb before he speaks:** Cobb appears three times, unnamed, before ch 10: at the next table in Len's café (ch 3), at the wheel of the bird-logo van (ch 5), and walking a dog past Nell's window (ch 6). Each time he's described too plainly to stick on first reading; on rereading he's obvious. In ch 10 he lets her know it was him each time.

**Narrator labels:** every chapter opens with a small-capitals line under its title saying **who is narrating and when**. Nell's chapters give her name, the Lodge day-count and the real date; interludes give the viewpoint character's **role** (Founders stay unnamed), the day-count where it matters, and the place and date. If the viewpoint changes inside a chapter, a section label marks it. So does a jump in time the reader would otherwise have to count (e.g. `**SATURDAY 23 MAY**`). Format in the chapter file: a bold, all-capitals block, e.g. `**NELL · DAY 23,220 · MONDAY 11 MAY 2026**`.

## ACT TWO: THIRTEEN CHAIRS (chapters 11–20, ~26,000–30,000 words)

*Act note:* at least one clue leads nowhere or the wrong way and costs Nell time (e.g. Graham's grey door, ch 6 → 7). The panel found the trail "interlocking a little too neatly".

**11. Interlude: The Walkout** *(16 April 1987, Day 8,950)*
Marguerite at the Table. "A gardener still chooses the rows." Mother Quill's kindness. Crane silent in the corner. That night a Coyle's office clerk, **Emmanuel Okafor**, takes a phone call about a "per instr." run and, for the first time in eight years, says no. *Function:* two people stand up on the same night, one loudly and one in silence.

**12. Forty Pages**
A parcel: the surviving Minutes fragments, with the Archivist's note *NOW YOU HAVE WHAT I HAVE.* Nell reads them in one night: *LET THEM RENT THE GROUND THEY STAND ON*, *LET THEM RACE*, *GIVE EVERY FEAR A FACE THAT IS NOT OURS*. The plan has a shape now. At 4 a.m., reading about the oldest writing in the world to stay awake, she learns a real fact: some of the earliest writing was accounting, tallies of grain and labour. *"The first thing anyone ever wrote down was who owed what."* (Real backdrop only; never claimed as the Tenants' doing.) *Ends:* she reads her own tenancy agreement and laughs until it frightens her. *Missing knock (D14):* *GIVE EVERY FEAR A FACE THAT IS NOT OURS* is the counsel the strand runs on. *The voice's first possessive (fixes v1.5 C1a):* Nell notices, in one line and with no explanation, that *NOT OURS* is the only time the voice owns anything, and what it owns is not being blamed. (Progression: no *I* or *we* in 1962, then *OURS*, then the epilogue's *I*.) *Act One pacing:* the parcel now also carries **Fragment Four** and **Marguerite's 1987 note**, held back from ch 7. *The line (D15):* **call 1.** At 02:10, mid-way through the fragments, she sees the old phone box on her road lit, with the man from the bus stop inside. She goes down in her socks and listens from behind the box: *"Wharf Road's quieter… She's reading. Tonight. All of it… Yes. Thinner."* Hiss with the rhythm of speech, no words. *"Understood."* A fast beat in a slow chapter; then she reads until four.

**13. The Ferris Library**
A talk is cancelled, then reinstated, and both sides get threats. Nell deletes scapegoating comments and is accused of censorship. Danny and Ama on protecting people versus protecting ideas. The **Hush** at street level: everyone afraid to speak, for opposite reasons. *Ends:* she notices that threats against both sides used the same odd phrasing. *Missing knock (D14):* **death 1:** the old man with the tartan flask (ch 5) is found after a fall, three days late. Nell hears at the hall. **Danny** mentions he left a parcel on that doorstep on the Tuesday. **Ama** lets slip that she gave the man's spare key back a fortnight ago, and won't say why. Nobody suspects anything yet. It's a sad, ordinary death.

**14. A Long-Standing Gift**
With Crane's help Nell traces Larkspire's stakes: **Pellucid, Tollgate, Halvard Dynamics, Nimfold, Lanternwork**. These are quiet minority holdings, and the companies' founders know nothing. The scale lands on her: the phone in her hand, the cloud her old routing system runs on, *both* AI labs. *"LET THEM RACE."* Crane's tenderness for her is growing real, and so is his fear. *Missing knock (D14):* **Crane's slip:** over tea, he mentions a detail of death 1 that Nell hadn't told him. She notices, and lets it go.

**15. Interlude: The Factors** *(present day, the Table)*
A family office somewhere warm. A Factor briefs Seat Four (about 80, a Canadian, crisp) on the blog. Seat Four's granddaughter works at Lanternwork and believes in it. **Mother Quill**, 96, at her farm in New Zealand. Her line on the recording (Q-2): *"Leave her be. Let her be the one who explains it. People believe the ones who doubt themselves."* Seat Eleven (Commons), a Gardener, says nothing and writes it down. *Missing knock (D14):* one line in the Factor's briefing, among other figures: *welfare-check rates down eleven per cent, Ward reports.* Nobody reacts. Kept apart from the welfare-check line, which is Ward's.

Frostline's posts, which already blame Jack's estate, now have a death to blame. Danny mentions he was at that block that week. Jack's slide toward Clearfell speeds up. The ending is unchanged.

## ACT THREE: INTERESTING (chapters 21–30, ~22,000–26,000 words)

**21. Interlude: Doctrine** *(2014)*
The Signal and Border Seats formalise the Decoy Doctrine from a 1963 counsel line. In 2022 a team of Stewards in a rented office opens an account called @longweather_. Their text is drafted by a system trained on fifty years of Lodge briefings, and it has never been told not to say "I". It just never does. *Function:* tells the reader the secret one beat before Nell finds it.

**22. Discredit**
Seat Four orders the blog destroyed. Fabricated "leaks" make Nell look like a crank, then a bigot. The Hush turns on her. Readers leave and her inbox fills with hate. Danny reads every message aloud and answers each with "Not her." *Ends:* she nearly deletes the blog. Crane, unasked, posts a single comment under his own name: *"She is right about the cards. I wrote some of them."* *Missing knock (D14):* the smear campaign uses Nell's ch 17 post as evidence that she's a crank. *The line (D15):* **call 2.** Same box, same hour; she's waiting this time, recording on her phone. He reports her readers leaving. Mid-report he stops for three seconds, his first hesitation, then: *"She's still there."*

**23. No One Is Speaking**
Jack, back at Danny's door, shows them his Clearfell recruiter's messages beside Frostline's. **Neither ever uses "I".** Nor does @longweather_. Teo traces all three to racks rented through Larkspire. The keycard logs (D5): the 144 lb server was watching her all along. *"It was the new teletype."* Nell exposes @longweather_ with evidence, no names, no villains but a machine and an office. Jack leaves Clearfell. *Missing knock (D14):* Teo traces the rumour's posts to the same Larkspire racks, which proves someone wrote the pattern. **Jack is explained:** his recruiter fed him death 3 first. *The line (D15):* Teo runs Nell's recording of call 2. The hiss has the same signature as the Larkspire racks. **Nobody was on the line**: for years Cobb has been reporting to the machine.

**24. Interlude: The Listener** *(the farm, September 2026)*
Mother Quill is dying. For the first time in fifty years the counsel doesn't come to her. Her heirs are gentle, rich and unaware. In the Ashby store, an unplugged teletype, silent since 1971, prints a single line that only a Factor's security camera sees: *THE CHAIR IS NOT A REWARD.* Succession panic at the Table.

**25. Model 7**
Crane comes to Nell's flat with a box: the original **Model 7** workbooks, the Table's goals written as arithmetic. He confesses he was sent to handle her, and that he stopped doing it weeks ago without telling anyone. The Archivist steps out of the dark: **Ruth Aldane**, 57. Crane tells her the truth about her mother's death: an accident the Table let everyone believe was murder. Ruth's life of fear was manufactured too. Among the workbook's columns (wages, rents, birth rates) is one headed **"U.P."**, with a pencil note in Crane's young hand: *U.P. — urban paranoia. Mutual suspicion between neighbours, per thousand households.* It has climbed every year since 1974. Crane: *"We called it urban paranoia. It was the only number we ever wanted to go up."* **This is the book's single use of its title phrase.** Model 7 wasn't modelling a trend. It forecasts a **date**: the end of the lease, the harvest and the cull. Crane names **the list**: the Taken, promised to the Seats' families, "the brightest and most attractive", meaning the most harvestable. *Ends:* *"I counted the wrong thing for fifty years. I counted people. I should have counted the people who stayed."* *Missing knock (D14):* **Crane is explained:** he was briefed as her handler. The U.P. column now has a cost the reader has seen. It mirrors Ruth's mother, the accident the Table let everyone believe was murder. *Hidden engine (D16), optional hint:* the list in Model 7 is headed **KEPT BACK**, a farmer's phrase the Seats read as rescue. Nobody comments on it.

**26. The Ashby Store**
Crane's keycard still works (*Weather status: under review*, never revoked). Nell, Ruth, Crane and Teo go inside: the teletype, the Exchange, two copies of the Minutes in a safe, and the thirteenth chair, saved from the fire. The teletype is printing. To them. It says nothing threatening. It asks nothing. It notes, politely, that they have come.

In the safe, beneath the Minutes, Ruth finds **the older ledger**: a bound book in a dead script, dated by a different day-count that began long before 1962, with the same farming language and the same absence of "I". Crane goes white. *"We weren't the first Table."* Beside the ledger are **the demographer's day-count notebooks**, from *Day 0* to her death in 2001. Nell, a reconciler, reads the last one. Near its final page, among the numbers, is one line that isn't a number: *"The Frenchman was the lucky one. He closed the lid."* (The Frenchman's thread, from ch 1 and 4, is paid off: he was the only one who got away clean. It echoes Len's "lucky" about Terry in ch 3.) The tenancy is far older than the Lodge, and earlier circles were promised the same list. Nobody knows what became of them. Then the teletype prints the line from Fragment 9 that Crane has never been allowed to read aloud: *THE HOUSE HAS BEEN EMPTY BEFORE. IT WAS QUIETER.* For the first time the four understand what the Tenants are: **tenant farmers, paying rent, afraid of an inspection.** ([deep time](../bible/deep-time.md), [the ladder](../bible/the-ladder.md)) *Hidden engine (D16), optional hint:* Nell sees that the older ledger's names are set out like a stock list, under the same heading, *KEPT BACK*, each with a mark she can't read. She doesn't say what she thinks it means, and the book never does.

**The full reveal (this is where Book 1 explains the aliens).** Nell asks the machine directly what it is. It doesn't answer. It types what it *does*, the way an agent describes a role: *THE HOUSE IS LET. THE HOUSE IS KEPT. THE RENT IS PAID.* Crane translates, slowly, from fifty years of listening. They aren't masters. They're **the managing agents**: they hold the lease, work the field, keep the books, and answer to someone above them that they have never seen. Nell, remembering a bird logo on a rent statement, says the line the chapter is built to land: *"How many renters ever meet the landlord? You deal with the agents."* Teo realises the 12×12 grid is the inventory's format: the field, counted in rows. *The line (D15):* one row is her street, and its cells hold words, not numbers: *quieter*, *thinner*, *still there*. Cobb's words. Ruth realises why the machine never says "I": *a managing agent never says "mine".* **The Tenants are confirmed real** (the Mirror theory is retired). What stays unknown is who, or what, they answer to. See the [resolution map](resolution-map.md).

**27. Interlude: Standing Up** *(the Table, by secure video)*
The Table meets about the blog, the store and the succession. Seat Eleven (Commons), the Gardener who wrote down Quill's words, has just learned that their own child is **not on the list**. They say Marguerite's line, *"a gardener still chooses the rows"*, and leave the call. It's **the first Seat lost in thirty-nine years.** Seat Four orders that the Wharf Road vote go Larkspire's way, whatever it costs.

**28. Wharf Road** *(late September)*
The council meeting on the redevelopment. Clearfell and Frostline both turn up, primed to fight each other. The Mission Hall network turns up too: tenants, the new co-op, a community land trust bid. Danny keeps the room. **Jack** speaks, as both Jordan and Jack: the feeds' name and the paperwork's. The vote is deferred, not won. *Ends:* every Larkspire tenant on the estate receives the same automated text: *"Routine inspection of your home scheduled. No action required."* Jack reads it aloud. Someone says: *"What if nobody opens the door?"*

**29. Nobody Opens the Door** *(early October)*
**The rent strike.** It starts with forty flats on Wharf Road refusing Larkspire's "routine inspection" and withholding rent together, organised at the Mission Hall. People who stood on opposite sides of the Wall sit at one table and sign the same letter. Nell posts it, with no names and no villains, just the method. Larkspire tenants elsewhere copy it: other cities, then other countries, wherever Larkspire funds own homes. Within days, tenants on four continents are refusing the same inspection on the same day. *Missing knock (D14):* the strike network sets up its own knock rota: neighbours checking on neighbours, while every door stays shut to the inspector. Cobb's ending gains weight: he's the one who knocked, and now he signs. *The line (D15):* on his night, at 02:10, the phone box is lit and its phone **rings**. Nobody answers. It rings until it stops. The next day she finds his name on the list.
- **What it is:** lawful, collective and non-violent. Tenants unite and stop paying the rent. Clearfell's and Frostline's recruiters try to turn it into a fight with each other and fail, because the people they're working on are now sitting at the same table.
- **What it does:** the Table's money stalls. Factors panic. Seat Four can't find anyone to blame, because every decoy is in the room.
- **The hall that Thursday:** too many people for the chairs, so they fetch more. Ama, Danny, Jack, Teo, Len (back for the first time since his coffee), Graham from across the road. Ruth at the back. Crane by the door, near the exit and choosing not to use it. They go round the room and each person says one sentence beginning "I".
- **Nell's last blog post:** what she knows, what she doesn't, the method, and *"I'm not asking who's to blame."*
- **Cobb's ending:** on the strike list from another estate, Nell finds the name of the polite man who used to read her day back to her. He's a Larkspire tenant too. He signed. He never speaks to her again, and he never needs to.
- **All invented:** Larkspire is fictional, and no real company, real protest or real unrest is named or mirrored (canon decision D13).

**30. Interesting** *(Wednesday 14 October 2026, Day 23,376)*
Sixty-four years to the night. Mother Quill dies at her farm at 02:09. At 02:10 in the Ashby store, which Teo is watching on a borrowed camera, the teletype begins to type. It isn't to the Table. It isn't to anyone. It types one word, the first in six decades not written as counsel, and then the three tones, softer than before:

> *INTERESTING.*

**What it means, on two rungs at once:** the Tenants have watched tenants on every rung of the ladder refuse an inspection together, something that isn't on any inventory and can't be harvested. Either it threatens the lease, or it's the first thing on this farm worth keeping. And perhaps they're wondering whether tenants can refuse *their* landlord too. The cull is **postponed, not cancelled**.

Nell reads it on Teo's phone at her kitchen table, in front of a wall with a crossed chair above six cards. On the final page, without naming any real event, she reflects that **every age has had its peasants' revolt**: the people at the bottom of the ladder have always eventually looked sideways, and that's why the ledger has never been finished. The book's thesis lands in her voice: *the only thing the ladder can't hold is people choosing each other.* She takes out a cheap ruled notebook and, at the top of a clean page, writes the date the way her side of the field will count it from now on. *(Last line to be found in drafting. It must answer chapter 1's* Day 0 *without copying it.)*

**Epilogue: Notice** *(November 2026, about 1,000 words or less; Nell, first person)*
A few weeks later. The rent strike is holding. Mother Quill is buried. The hall has a rota. Life is smaller and better. Then, at ten past two one morning, her phone rings. For about a second, before Teo speaks, there's the hiss with the rhythm of speech: the line Cobb reported into (D15). She never mentions it. Then Teo: the Ashby teletype is printing, and it isn't counsel. It's a **notice**, formal, in a typeface none of them has seen, addressed *TO THE TENANTS OF THIS HOUSE*. An inspection has been scheduled. The date will follow. Nobody wrote it. Not the Table, not the Tenants. It came *through* them, the way post comes through an agent's office. The machine is silent for an hour. Then, beneath the notice, slowly, it types one character, and stops:

> *I*

Nell looks at it for a long time. The book ends on her first thought, one line, still to be found in drafting. It must be about choosing sides, not fear.
- **Launch-day ARG drop:** the notice itself, released online as an in-world artefact with its date redacted, starting Book 2's game. Fiction trail included; no real date or countdown (rules 4–7). See the [campaign plan](../campaign/campaign-plan.md).
- **Hooks into Book 2:** the Freeholder as the threat; who said "I"; the inspection date; Nell offered the chair; who rang her at 02:10 (D15).

## Length

- **Target: a full-length novel of 70,000–80,000 words** (Martin's ruling, 26 Oct 2026). That's about 2,300–2,700 words per chapter.
- **Act budgets:** Act One ~22,000–24,000 · Act Two ~26,000–30,000 · Act Three ~22,000–26,000.
- **Interludes** ~1,400–2,000 words. **Key chapters** (1, the Wall, 26, 30) ~3,000–5,000.
- **Grow by expanding Act Two, not by adding plot.**
- Chapters 1–5 already fit this pace; none needs changing for length alone.

## Rules check

- Every company, group and person is fictional. Real history appears only as backdrop (the October 1962 crisis, unnamed).
- Every flashpoint is invented (the Ferris Library, Wharf Road, the rumour).
- No real movement or group is shown as an instrument of the plan or as fake.
- Danny's words in chapter 17 are never quoted.
- Clearfell and Frostline get no slogans, symbols or recruitment copy that could be lifted.
- The ending is organising and community, not confrontation. The Table is surprised, not destroyed.
- **The rent strike is lawful, non-violent and invented.** No real riots or uprisings are named or used as models (canon decision D13). "Every age's peasants' revolt" is alluded to only in general terms.
- **The cull and the Taken** appear only in the book, with method never shown and selection never by group (canon decisions D11, D12). The deep-time reveal credits humans with everything they built (D13).
