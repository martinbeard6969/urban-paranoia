# Urban Paranoia: chapter outline

**Shape:** 30 chapters in three acts, plus a short epilogue. Present-day chapters (spring–autumn 2026) are in **Nell's first person**. Seven **interludes** step back into the Table's history in close third person, each a short, self-contained story that shifts how the reader sees the present. The book opens on **Day 0** (14 Oct 1962) and closes on the 64th anniversary, **Day 23,376** (Wed 14 Oct 2026).

**The spine:** Nell moves from *collecting evidence* (Act 1), to *seeing the shape* (Act 2, ending at the Wall), to *acting with other people* (Act 3). The Table is never defeated. It's **surprised**.

**Rhythm rule:** whenever the Tenants speak, long observed paragraphs are broken by single centred lines of counsel (as in chapter 1).

**Narrator labels:** every chapter opens with a small-capitals line under its title saying **who is narrating and when**. Nell's chapters give her name, the Lodge day-count and the real date; interludes give the viewpoint character's **role** (Founders stay unnamed), the day-count where it matters, and the place and date. If the viewpoint changes inside a chapter, a section label marks it. Format in the chapter file: a bold, all-capitals block, e.g. `**NELL · DAY 23,220 · MONDAY 11 MAY 2026**`.

## ACT ONE: THE LONG WEATHER (chapters 1–10, ~22,000–24,000 words)

**1. Day Zero** *(1962; interlude-style opener; drafted v2.2, ~3,000)*
The Coyle's driver delivers the teletype. Seven mid-level officials come to a seminar. At 02:10 it begins: *SIT. THERE IS TIME.* Four leave; three stay for the prediction. The demographer writes *Day 0*. → [chapter file](chapters/01-day-zero.md)

**2. Contribution to the Transition** *(April 2026, Day 23,199; drafted v1.1, ~2,500)* → [chapter file](chapters/02-contribution-to-the-transition.md)
Nell's redundancy, told through the croissants and the letter. The archive by the rail yard; the sorting eye. The 1971 manifest: no weight, no charge, *D. 3062*, the purple stamp. That night she starts a blog, because writing things down is the one thing left. *Ends:* she hits "publish" and the post sits there, unread, *"like a lantern in a field."*

**3. Reconciliation** *(drafted v1.1, ~2,500)* → [chapter file](chapters/03-reconciliation.md)
Vehicle logs, the 1962 box, the driver's note (chapter 1's words, seen again sixty-four years later; readers feel the click). Day 0 solved. **Len** over coffee: the cash man, the wardrobe that hummed, Terry. *Ends:* *"You notice it for forty years."*

**4. Interlude: Not Far, Only Elsewhere** *(2 March 1971, Day 3,061; drafted v1.1, ~1,450)* → [chapter file](chapters/04-not-far-only-elsewhere.md)
The last session at Harrowgate before the machine is moved. The three Founders, now quietly wealthy from their first container-shipping stakes, uneasy at how easy it was. The machine prints *NOT FAR. ONLY ELSEWHERE.* (recording HG-71). Cut to Le Havre: the French actuary plays three notes on his piano and closes the lid forever. *Function:* shows the Table's first riches and first guilt. They were nobodies once.

**5. The Sensible Voice** *(drafted v2.1, ~2,800)* → [chapter file](chapters/05-the-sensible-voice.md)
**Danny** in Nell's kitchen: "It's a tax dodge, Nell." The **Mission Hall** and **Pastor Ama**. **Jack Prentice ("Jordan")**, 19, his apprenticeship cut and his mum's flat on the Wharf Road list. The first **@longweather_** comments, described and deleted. *Ends:* Danny's line, "Don't let anyone tell you it's the people down your street," spoken while a Larkspire letting agent's van idles outside.

**6. Count Forwards** *(drafted v1, ~2,250)* → [chapter file](chapters/06-count-forwards.md)
The line in the source code at 03:12. The typed letter. Day 22,861: the croissants again. **144 lb.** Her father, the man who knew where everything was. *Ends:* *"I'm going to stop this paragraph here,"* as she stares at her father's crossword dictionary.

**7. The Grey Door**
The colour-pick. `/6b6e70`. Fragment Four, Marguerite's note, HG-68. The tones hit Nell physically, through cheap laptop speakers: the same "too pure" sound as chapter 1. *Ends:* *EVEN FOR THE ONES WHO ANSWERED THE TELEPHONE.*

**8. Interlude: Liaison** *(1979)*
Hollis Crane, 34, is brought to Harrowgate as the new liaison and sits with the machine for the first time. He's dazzled. He does the arithmetic overnight and is proud of it by dawn. That week a Canadian insurance regulator is seated as Seat Four. *Function:* makes Crane sympathetic, and culpable, before Nell meets him.

**9. Card 0144**
The Harlow-Vane site, the card, the counter climbing (*"viewed 1 time"* → 400 → 9,000). Crane's first email. They meet for tea in a station café: hymns, kindness, precise memory. He gives her context she couldn't find alone. *Dramatic irony at chapter end:* Crane, on the train home, phones a number and tells a polite voice (a **Factor**) everything she said.

**10. Canaries**
**Teo**, the night technician at DC-3: 12×12 grids in the error logs, power drawn by "idle" racks rented next to Lanternwork's. Nell's Weather report on the hiring gap. Then **Cobb**, at a bus stop, kindly reciting her Tuesday back to her. *Ends Act One:* she realises she's being **watched**, and that the watchers aren't hiding it.

## ACT TWO: THIRTEEN CHAIRS (chapters 11–20, ~26,000–30,000 words)

**11. Interlude: The Walkout** *(16 April 1987, Day 8,950)*
Marguerite at the Table. "A gardener still chooses the rows." Mother Quill's kindness. Crane silent in the corner. That night a Coyle's office clerk, **Emmanuel Okafor**, takes a phone call about a "per instr." run and, for the first time in eight years, says no. *Function:* two people stand up on the same night, one loudly and one in silence.

**12. Forty Pages**
A parcel: the surviving Minutes fragments, with the Archivist's note *NOW YOU HAVE WHAT I HAVE.* Nell reads them in one night: *LET THEM RENT THE GROUND THEY STAND ON*, *LET THEM RACE*, *GIVE EVERY FEAR A FACE THAT IS NOT OURS*. The plan has a shape now. At 4 a.m., reading about the oldest writing in the world to stay awake, she learns a real fact: some of the earliest writing was accounting, tallies of grain and labour. *"The first thing anyone ever wrote down was who owed what."* (Real backdrop only; never claimed as the Tenants' doing.) *Ends:* she reads her own tenancy agreement and laughs until it frightens her.

**13. The Ferris Library**
A talk is cancelled, then reinstated, and both sides get threats. Nell deletes scapegoating comments and is accused of censorship. Danny and Ama on protecting people versus protecting ideas. The **Hush** at street level: everyone afraid to speak, for opposite reasons. *Ends:* she notices that threats against both sides used the same odd phrasing.

**14. A Long-Standing Gift**
With Crane's help Nell traces Larkspire's stakes: **Pellucid, Tollgate, Halvard Dynamics, Nimfold, Lanternwork**. These are quiet minority holdings, and the companies' founders know nothing. The scale lands on her: the phone in her hand, the cloud her old routing system runs on, *both* AI labs. *"LET THEM RACE."* Crane's tenderness for her is growing real, and so is his fear.

**15. Interlude: The Factors** *(present day, the Table)*
A family office somewhere warm. A Factor briefs Seat Four (about 80, a Canadian, crisp) on the blog. Seat Four's granddaughter works at Lanternwork and believes in it. **Mother Quill**, 96, at her farm in New Zealand. Her line on the recording (Q-2): *"Leave her be. Let her be the one who explains it. People believe the ones who doubt themselves."* Seat Eleven (Commons), a Gardener, says nothing and writes it down.

**16. Clearfell**
Jack is drawn into **Clearfell**: anger at landlords, a recruiter who listens. Danny finds out. Frostline posts blame Jack's estate. Two groups, two enemies, same tone. Nell and Danny can't reach him. *Ends:* Jack: "At least they're *doing* something."

**17. The Rumour**
A crime that never happened, a different villain in every feed, and "Jordan Pryce" named in one. A crowd outside the Mission Hall. Danny in the middle, hearing himself say something (never quoted). Ama holds the door. Nobody is hurt, but it was close. *Ends:* Danny walks home without his bike and doesn't notice. **Ama's back room** (set up in ch 5, where she quietly takes in the young woman in sunglasses) becomes the refuge during the confrontation: the people the rumour targets are sheltered there while Ama holds the door.

**18. The Wall** *(MIDPOINT)*
Six cards, the counsel, the names, the threads that only run sideways. "Where's their name?" The crossed chair above the row. Confessions. 3:40 a.m.: **Larkspire Property Services** will inspect her flat. Jack Prentice and Jordan Pryce: *"one name for the feeds, one for the paperwork."* She texts Ama to book the hall. → [scene outline](act-2/scene-the-wall.md)

**19. Routine Inspection**
10:00. Two men from Larkspire "property services". One of them is Cobb, who admires her wall, which she didn't take down. He leaves a card with no name on it. Then the Archivist's post: **card 0212**. *Reliable. Asks no questions. Declined further work following S.7 matter.* Her father **stopped**. And: *Daughter: Coyle's analyst post. Placement noted.* Was her whole career arranged? *Ends:* she can't tell, and that's the worst of it.

**20. Thursday**
The first Mission Hall meeting: eleven people, bad tea, a broken urn, awkwardness, then something. Neighbours from opposite sides of the Wall sit at one table, which the Outrage Engine exists to prevent. Jack doesn't come. Nell posts *"Every arrow on my wall points sideways"*, with no names. *Ends Act Two:* the post spreads, and at a family office somewhere warm a Factor flags it red.

## ACT THREE: INTERESTING (chapters 21–30, ~22,000–26,000 words)

**21. Interlude: Doctrine** *(2014)*
The Signal and Border Seats formalise the Decoy Doctrine from a 1963 counsel line. In 2022 a team of Stewards in a rented office opens an account called @longweather_. Their text is drafted by a system trained on fifty years of Lodge briefings, and it has never been told not to say "I". It just never does. *Function:* tells the reader the secret one beat before Nell finds it.

**22. Discredit**
Seat Four orders the blog destroyed. Fabricated "leaks" make Nell look like a crank, then a bigot. The Hush turns on her. Readers leave and her inbox fills with hate. Danny reads every message aloud and answers each with "Not her." *Ends:* she nearly deletes the blog. Crane, unasked, posts a single comment under his own name: *"She is right about the cards. I wrote some of them."*

**23. No One Is Speaking**
Jack, back at Danny's door, shows them his Clearfell recruiter's messages beside Frostline's. **Neither ever uses "I".** Nor does @longweather_. Teo traces all three to racks rented through Larkspire. The keycard logs (D5): the 144 lb server was watching her all along. *"It was the new teletype."* Nell exposes @longweather_ with evidence, no names, no villains but a machine and an office. Jack leaves Clearfell.

**24. Interlude: The Listener** *(the farm, September 2026)*
Mother Quill is dying. For the first time in fifty years the counsel doesn't come to her. Her heirs are gentle, rich and unaware. In the Ashby store, an unplugged teletype, silent since 1971, prints a single line that only a Factor's security camera sees: *THE CHAIR IS NOT A REWARD.* Succession panic at the Table.

**25. Model 7**
Crane comes to Nell's flat with a box: the original **Model 7** workbooks, the Table's goals written as arithmetic. He confesses he was sent to handle her, and that he stopped doing it weeks ago without telling anyone. The Archivist steps out of the dark: **Ruth Aldane**, 57. Crane tells her the truth about her mother's death: an accident the Table let everyone believe was murder. Ruth's life of fear was manufactured too. Among the workbook's columns (wages, rents, birth rates) is one headed **"U.P."**, with a pencil note in Crane's young hand: *U.P. — urban paranoia. Mutual suspicion between neighbours, per thousand households.* It has climbed every year since 1974. Crane: *"We called it urban paranoia. It was the only number we ever wanted to go up."* **This is the book's single use of its title phrase.** Model 7 wasn't modelling a trend. It forecasts a **date**: the end of the lease, the harvest and the cull. Crane names **the list**: the Taken, promised to the Seats' families, "the brightest and most attractive", meaning the most harvestable. *Ends:* *"I counted the wrong thing for fifty years. I counted people. I should have counted the people who stayed."*

**26. The Ashby Store**
Crane's keycard still works (*Weather status: under review*, never revoked). Nell, Ruth, Crane and Teo go inside: the teletype, the Exchange, two copies of the Minutes in a safe, and the thirteenth chair, saved from the fire. The teletype is printing. To them. It says nothing threatening. It asks nothing. It notes, politely, that they have come.

In the safe, beneath the Minutes, Ruth finds **the older ledger**: a bound book in a dead script, dated by a different day-count that began long before 1962, with the same farming language and the same absence of "I". Crane goes white. *"We weren't the first Table."* Beside the ledger are **the demographer's day-count notebooks**, from *Day 0* to her death in 2001. Nell, a reconciler, reads the last one. Near its final page, among the numbers, is one line that isn't a number: *"The Frenchman was the lucky one. He closed the lid."* (The Frenchman's thread, from ch 1 and 4, is paid off: he was the only one who got away clean. It echoes Len's "lucky" about Terry in ch 3.) The tenancy is far older than the Lodge, and earlier circles were promised the same list. Nobody knows what became of them. Then the teletype prints the line from Fragment 9 that Crane has never been allowed to read aloud: *THE HOUSE HAS BEEN EMPTY BEFORE. IT WAS QUIETER.* For the first time the four understand what the Tenants are: **tenant farmers, paying rent, afraid of an inspection.** ([deep time](../bible/deep-time.md), [the ladder](../bible/the-ladder.md))

**The full reveal (this is where Book 1 explains the aliens).** Nell asks the machine directly what it is. It doesn't answer. It types what it *does*, the way an agent describes a role: *THE HOUSE IS LET. THE HOUSE IS KEPT. THE RENT IS PAID.* Crane translates, slowly, from fifty years of listening. They aren't masters. They're **the managing agents**: they hold the lease, work the field, keep the books, and answer to someone above them that they have never seen. Nell, remembering a bird logo on a rent statement, says the line the chapter is built to land: *"How many renters ever meet the landlord? You deal with the agents."* Teo realises the 12×12 grid is the inventory's format: the field, counted in rows. Ruth realises why the machine never says "I": *a managing agent never says "mine".* **The Tenants are confirmed real** (the Mirror theory is retired). What stays unknown is who, or what, they answer to. See the [resolution map](resolution-map.md).

**27. Interlude: Standing Up** *(the Table, by secure video)*
The Table meets about the blog, the store and the succession. Seat Eleven (Commons), the Gardener who wrote down Quill's words, has just learned that their own child is **not on the list**. They say Marguerite's line, *"a gardener still chooses the rows"*, and leave the call. It's **the first Seat lost in thirty-nine years.** Seat Four orders that the Wharf Road vote go Larkspire's way, whatever it costs.

**28. Wharf Road** *(late September)*
The council meeting on the redevelopment. Clearfell and Frostline both turn up, primed to fight each other. The Mission Hall network turns up too: tenants, the new co-op, a community land trust bid. Danny keeps the room. **Jack** speaks, as both Jordan and Jack: the feeds' name and the paperwork's. The vote is deferred, not won. *Ends:* every Larkspire tenant on the estate receives the same automated text: *"Routine inspection of your home scheduled. No action required."* Jack reads it aloud. Someone says: *"What if nobody opens the door?"*

**29. Nobody Opens the Door** *(early October)*
**The rent strike.** It starts with forty flats on Wharf Road refusing Larkspire's "routine inspection" and withholding rent together, organised at the Mission Hall. People who stood on opposite sides of the Wall sit at one table and sign the same letter. Nell posts it, with no names and no villains, just the method. Larkspire tenants elsewhere copy it: other cities, then other countries, wherever Larkspire funds own homes. Within days, tenants on four continents are refusing the same inspection on the same day.
- **What it is:** lawful, collective and non-violent. Tenants unite and stop paying the rent. Clearfell's and Frostline's recruiters try to turn it into a fight with each other and fail, because the people they're working on are now sitting at the same table.
- **What it does:** the Table's money stalls. Factors panic. Seat Four can't find anyone to blame, because every decoy is in the room.
- **The hall that Thursday:** too many people for the chairs, so they fetch more. Ama, Danny, Jack, Teo, Len (back for the first time since his coffee), Graham from across the road. Ruth at the back. Crane by the door, near the exit and choosing not to use it. They go round the room and each person says one sentence beginning "I".
- **Nell's last blog post:** what she knows, what she doesn't, the method, and *"I'm not asking who's to blame."*
- **Cobb's ending:** on the strike list from another estate, Nell finds the name of the polite man who used to read her day back to her. He's a Larkspire tenant too. He signed. He never speaks to her again, and he never needs to.
- **All invented:** Larkspire is fictional, and no real company, real protest or real unrest is named or mirrored (canon decision D13).

**30. Interesting** *(Wednesday 14 October 2026, Day 23,376)*
Sixty-four years to the night. Mother Quill dies at her farm at 02:09. At 02:10 in the Ashby store, which Teo is watching on a borrowed camera, the teletype begins to type. It isn't to the Table. It isn't to anyone. It types one word, the first in six decades not written as counsel, and then the three tones, softer than before:

> *INTERESTING.*

**What it means, on two rungs at once:** the Tenants have watched tenants on every rung of the ladder refuse an inspection together, something that isn't on any inventory and can't be harvested. Either it threatens the lease, or it's the first thing on this farm worth keeping. And perhaps they're wondering whether tenants can refuse *their* landlord too. The cull is **postponed, not cancelled**.

Nell reads it on Teo's phone at her kitchen table, in front of a wall with a crossed chair above six cards. On the final page, without naming any real event, she reflects that **every age has had its peasants' revolt**: the people at the bottom of the ladder have always eventually looked sideways, and that's why the ledger has never been finished. The book's thesis lands in her voice: *the only thing the ladder can't hold is people choosing each other.* She takes out a cheap ruled notebook and, at the top of a clean page, writes the date the way her side of the field will count it from now on. *(Last line to be found in drafting. It must answer chapter 1's* Day 0 *without copying it.)*

**Epilogue: Notice** *(November 2026, about 1,000 words or less; Nell, first person)*
A few weeks later. The rent strike is holding. Mother Quill is buried. The hall has a rota. Life is smaller and better. Then, at ten past two one morning, Teo phones: the Ashby teletype is printing, and it isn't counsel. It's a **notice**, formal, in a typeface none of them has seen, addressed *TO THE TENANTS OF THIS HOUSE*. An inspection has been scheduled. The date will follow. Nobody wrote it. Not the Table, not the Tenants. It came *through* them, the way post comes through an agent's office. The machine is silent for an hour. Then, beneath the notice, slowly, it types one character, and stops:

> *I*

Nell looks at it for a long time. The book ends on her first thought, one line, still to be found in drafting. It must be about choosing sides, not fear.
- **Launch-day ARG drop:** the notice itself, released online as an in-world artefact with its date redacted, starting Book 2's game. Fiction trail included; no real date or countdown (rules 4–7). See the [campaign plan](../campaign/campaign-plan.md).
- **Hooks into Book 2:** the Freeholder as the threat; who said "I"; the inspection date; Nell offered the chair.

## Length

- **Target: a full-length novel of 70,000–80,000 words** (Martin's ruling, 26 Oct 2026). That's about 2,300–2,700 words per chapter.
- **Act budgets:** Act One ~22,000–24,000 · Act Two ~26,000–30,000 · Act Three ~22,000–26,000.
- **Interludes** ~1,400–2,000 words. **Key chapters** (1, the Wall, 26, 30) ~3,000–5,000.
- **Grow by expanding Act Two, not by adding plot.**
- Chapters 1–5 already fit this pace; none needs changing for length alone.

## Rules check

- Every company, group and person is fictional. Real history appears only as backdrop (the October 1962 crisis, unnamed).
- Every flashpoint is invented (the Ferris Library, Wharf Road, the rumour).
- No real movement or group is shown as an instrument of the plan or as fake.
- Danny's words in chapter 17 are never quoted.
- Clearfell and Frostline get no slogans, symbols or recruitment copy that could be lifted.
- The ending is organising and community, not confrontation. The Table is surprised, not destroyed.
- **The rent strike is lawful, non-violent and invented.** No real riots or uprisings are named or used as models (canon decision D13). "Every age's peasants' revolt" is alluded to only in general terms.
- **The cull and the Taken** appear only in the book, with method never shown and selection never by group (canon decisions D11, D12). The deep-time reveal credits humans with everything they built (D13).
