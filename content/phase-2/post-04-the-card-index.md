# The Card Index

*Posted by Nell (Vesper)*

It took you nineteen hours to open the grey door. I'd have taken a month. Somebody colour-picked the band on the letter, typed six characters into an address bar, and sent me the link at four in the morning with no message, as if they'd found something they didn't want to be alone with. I understand that impulse better than I'd like to.

I've spent a week with what was behind it. I'll try not to tell you what to think. I'll tell you what I noticed.

---

## Three things behind a door

**A page of minutes from 1962.** Three people record, in the flat language of a committee, that a machine printed for forty-five minutes while it wasn't plugged in. One line is struck through in pencil, and they agree never to say a particular word outside the room. The word was "fewer". Minutes exist so that nobody can be blamed later. These read like people who knew they'd be blamed eventually and wanted it recorded that they'd been careful.

**A note from 1987,** with two coffee rings and a line I can't put down: *"I said a gardener still chooses the rows."* Whoever "M." was, she was frightened and she was brave. Those aren't opposites, whatever we're taught.

**A recording,** which I played through laptop speakers that make everything sound like it's happening in a biscuit tin. Hiss, a clock, a teletype. Then three notes: high, low, high. Everything else in that room sounded *recorded*: worn, roomy, real. Those three notes sounded too clean for the room they were recorded in, like a line drawn with a ruler across a page of handwriting. I've listened nine times. I'm going to stop.

---

## The counter

The door led to another door: a dusty website for an institute that closed in 2009, and an index card for a man called **Crane**. At the bottom is a line of grey text: *This card has been viewed 1 time(s) since digitisation.*

When the first of you found it, it said **1**. By morning it said **312**. As I write, it says **9,416**.

It's the most ordinary thing on the site, the kind of widget a retired academic adds because it came free. But a counter has a reader too. Somewhere, someone who keeps that archive "on a voluntary basis" has watched a number that sat at 1 for nine years climb to nine thousand in a week. I don't know if that frightened him or pleased him. I suspect he doesn't either.

---

## The back of the card

One line got me:

> *It is very easy to be proud of arithmetic. It never asks you what it is for.*

I believed it. I've been proud of arithmetic my whole working life. Numbers don't argue, and they don't ask what you've done with them.

But a few of you noticed what I did. Crane says he's been trying to answer "M." for **thirty-nine years**. Count forwards from 1987 and you get **this year**. That note wasn't written in 2017, when the site was last updated. It was written recently. And it ends: *"If you are reading this, someone has opened the grey door. Good."*

He knew the door would be opened. Either he's very good at waiting, or someone told him.

---

## The other card

Hidden in the dead drop's page source:

> *MS [—]. YOUR FATHER HAS A CARD TOO. I WOULD NOT LOOK AT THAT ONE YET.*

I'm a reconciler. You can guess how long I lasted. I tried the only number that made sense: my father's extension at Coyle's for thirty years, which I still know the way you know a childhood phone number. Two-one-two.

> *This card is withheld at the request of the family.*

I *am* the family. My mother has been gone eleven years. There's no one else, and I've never asked for anything to be withheld. So either it's a standard message on a lazy website, or someone decided on my behalf what I'm allowed to know about my own father.

---

## The sensible voice, again

D. listened to all of it in my kitchen, properly, the way he used to at union meetings.

> "A website with a counter isn't a confession, Nell. Some old fella's got a hobby, and you've given it nine thousand visitors."

Then, because he's D.: *"Don't write to him."*

---

## What I did

I wrote to him.

The site gives *c/o The Harlow-Vane Archive, PO Box 1962*: no town, no postcode. I don't think it's a real box. I think it's a door painted on a wall. So I wrote the letter by hand, telling him who I am, what I've found and what I'm afraid of, and then I didn't post it. It's in the drawer with the manifest. Some letters you write to find out what you'd say.

**Please don't write to that box yourselves, or try to find Dr. Crane.** He may be a lonely old man with a hobby. If he's anything more, it's my problem, not yours. If he wants to talk, he knows where I am. Apparently everybody does.

— N.
