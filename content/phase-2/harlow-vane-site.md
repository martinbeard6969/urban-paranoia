# The Harlow-Vane Institute for Systems Studies: holding site

## Concept

A dormant archive website for a research institute that closed in 2009. It looks built in 2011 and abandoned in 2017. It's earnest, a little vain, and almost deserted.

**The craft here is controlled dullness.** Every public page should read like something a real retired academic would maintain out of loyalty. Nothing on any single page is sinister. The dread builds up across them: in the gaps, in a publications list that reads like a plan once you've seen the plan, and in a card index that remembers everyone.

**In-world, the site is kept going by one volunteer: Dr. Hollis Crane, Honorary Archivist.** That's how he knows when card 0144 has been opened, and why he writes to Vesper.

## Design (for build)

- **Look:** an early-2010s institutional site. A white page, a narrow centred column (about 720px), a serif body face (e.g. *Source Serif* from Google Fonts), a navy header bar, and a small crest: a **sundial with no numerals**.
- **Wear and tear:** "Last updated: March 2017", one broken image on the History page, and a small visitor counter on each card page.
- **Mobile:** single column; the navigation collapses to a plain list.
- **Navigation:** Home · History · Publications · People · Card Index · Contact

## Page: History

> **Founding.** The Institute was established in 1958 by private endowment, "to study the long-term conditions of settled societies". Its early work concerned census methods, labour statistics and the then-new art of simulating an economy on a machine.
>
> **International seminars.** From 1960 the Institute hosted small residential seminars for promising officials from many countries, held at a leased country house where, as one participant recalled, "the fog came in at four and the conversation improved accordingly."
>
> **C.S.S.** From 1962 the Institute's work was organised around a small department known to staff only by its initials, "C.S.S.". The department built some of the earliest long-range social forecasting models in Europe, on equipment one former fellow called "louder than it was clever".
>
> *[Broken image: "css_teletype_room_1964.jpg"]*
>
> **Later years.** Through the 1980s and 1990s the Institute published widely on employment, household formation and demographic change, supported first by the Ashby Trust and later by a long-standing gift from Larkspire Mutual Re. Funding narrowed in the 2000s, and in 2009 the trustees resolved to wind up the Institute and pass its remaining assets to the Ashby Trust.
>
> **The archive.** Most of the paper archive went into storage at closure. A small part has been digitised, including the **staff card index**, which recorded every fellow, clerk and contractor the Institute ever engaged.

*Notes:*
- **"Known to staff only by its initials."** The site never spells out C.S.S. It's an obvious gap, and visitors will notice.
- **The international seminars** quietly confirm the multinational guest list of 1962 without naming anyone.
- **Ashby Trust and Larkspire** as funders link the Institute to the 1971 consignee and to the Lodge's money, on a page that looks like routine gratitude.

## PRODUCTION NOTES (out-of-world, do not publish)

**What changed in v2**
- The same pages, card logic, puzzles and canon as v1. The public prose now reads as convincing institutional dullness, and Crane's card note is sharpened to the literary standard.
- **New canon, consistent with the reworked Lodge:**
  - **"International seminars" for promising officials, held at a leased country house**, which confirms the multinational 1962 guests.
  - **Funding from the Ashby Trust and "a long-standing gift from Larkspire Mutual Re"**, a routine-looking line that ties Harlow-Vane to the Lodge's money.
- **New lines worth keeping:**
  - "the fog came in at four and the conversation improved accordingly"
  - "as time and eyesight allow"
  - "replies may be slow, but they will come"
  - "It is very easy to be proud of arithmetic. It never asks you what it is for."
  - "I have not found a way to say it that lets me sleep."
  - "Somebody had to."
- **A deliberate ambiguity:** Crane's back-of-card note is dated by its own arithmetic ("thirty-nine years" since 1987, so 2026) and anticipates "the grey door". So he wrote it recently, knowing the door would open. Was it remorse, or a lure? Canon decision D6 says both, in turn. Don't resolve it in the ARG.

**Name history:** this institute was first drafted as the "Calder Foundation" and renamed on 25 Oct 2026, because a real Calder Foundation exists (rule 3). Run trademark, charity-register and domain checks on **Harlow-Vane** before launch, as for every fictional name in the [pre-launch checklist](../../campaign/prelaunch-checklist.md).

**Canon (unchanged)**
- **C.S.S.** stands for "Carrying-capacity & Social Stability" internally; the cover was "Centre for Systems Studies".
- **Crane:** born 1945; joined 1973; H.H. liaison sitting in on contact sessions from 1979; Model 7; "reservations noted" in 1991; withdrew his paper in 2006; Weather status under review since 2009; Honorary Archivist since 2010.
- **"Re: Seat 7. No further contact."** in 1987: the Lodge cut Crane off from Marguerite.
