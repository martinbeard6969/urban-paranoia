# The Lodge

> "We were not chosen because we were the most powerful. We were chosen because we were the most willing."
> (attributed to "Founder Three", from the Harrowgate Minutes, 1963)

## Summary

The Lodge is a small, self-perpetuating, **multinational** circle of human intermediaries who have carried out the Tenants' advice since 1962. **Before contact they were nobodies:** competent, overlooked, mid-level officials. Sixty years of counsel about "which way the weather will turn" have made them, and their families, among the richest and most hidden people alive. It is **not** a government, a bank, a religion, an ethnic group or a political party. The Table has **thirteen chairs** but has never had more than **twelve seated members**: the thirteenth chair belongs to the Tenants (see [canon decisions D1](canon-decisions.md)). Members are drawn from no single nation, class or faith, and the Lodge deliberately avoids any identifying pattern so that it can never be traced.

Members never call it "the Lodge" themselves. Internally it is **"the Table"**. "The Lodge" is the outsider name Vesper picks up from a misfiled 1970s memo.

## Origins

- **Autumn 1962. The Harrowgate Contact.** The Harlow-Vane Institute hosts an invitation-only international seminar in "systems forecasting" at **Harrowgate House**, a fictional country estate in a fogbound valley. Its seven guests are **promising mid-level officials from seven countries**: clever enough to be invited, junior enough not to be missed. A Tenant makes contact through the house's experimental teletype (see [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md)). Four guests leave and remember nothing coherent. Three stay. They become the first three Founders.

### The seven guests (writers' room; names still open)

| | Nationality | Job in 1962 | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ONE** | American | Network planner at a regional telephone utility in the Midwest | Founder. Practical, impatient. d. 1976 |
| **TWO** | West German | Statistics officer at a federal economics ministry | Founder. The sceptic who checks the plug. d. 2001 |
| **THREE** | British | Civil-service demographer | Founder. Noticed the counsel never says "I". d. 2001 |
| Guest four | Japanese | Economist at a trade ministry | Left. Remembered nothing. |
| Guest five | Brazilian | Clerk at the currency office in Rio (Brazil's central bank wasn't founded until Dec 1964) | Left. Remembered nothing. |
| Guest six | Indian | Statistician at a national planning ministry | Left. Remembered nothing. |
| Guest seven | French | Actuary at a marine insurer | Left. Dreamt of the three tones for the rest of his life (book Act 3 flashback). |

**Rule:** the guests' real employers are never named. Their jobs stay generic, and none is modelled on a real person.
- **1963. The Harrowgate Minutes.** The Founders write a 40-page record of the Tenants' "counsel". Only fragments survive in-world. They are the most important artefacts in the ARG.
- **1964–1969. Seating the Table.** The Founders recruit nine more members over five years, all mid-level people (a shipping-line manager, a broadcasting engineer, a housing official, a junior diplomat, a pensions administrator and others) from Europe, the Americas, Asia, Africa and Oceania. Each one is approached at a point of personal crisis. Recruitment always takes the form of an *offer of certainty*.

## Then and now: how the Table got rich

The Tenants never told the Table to get rich. They told it **what was coming**: containers, computers, networks, phones, the move to buying everything online, batteries, machines that think. People who know which way the weather will turn, sixty years early, don't stay mid-level for long.

- **1960s–70s:** small, early stakes in container shipping, telecoms and the first personal-computer firms, through the Ashby Trust and nominee accounts.
- **1980s–2000s:** **Larkspire Mutual Re** becomes the main vehicle. Each Seat's family sets up a **family office**.
- **2000s–now:** quiet, minority stakes, never controlling, never public, in the fictional global companies that shape daily life. See [locations & organisations](locations-and-organisations.md): **Pellucid** (devices), **Tollgate** (online marketplace and cloud), **Halvard Dynamics** (electric vehicles, batteries, satellites), and **both** of the leading AI labs, **Nimfold** and **Lanternwork**. The Table funds both sides of the AI race: *"LET THEM RACE."*
- **The companies' founders and executives are not Lodge members** and don't know it exists. The Table owns pieces of what they build and nudges at the edges: a board seat filled by a family-office nominee, a funding round arriving at the right moment. That keeps the Lodge invisible and makes it more frightening.

### The families
- Every Seat holder's family is now very rich: houses on four continents, foundations, art, schools. Most relatives **don't know where the money's compass comes from**. They're told the family "has always been lucky with timing".
- Seats aren't hereditary (canon decision D1), but the Table prefers to seat people raised close to it. Several current Seats are children or in-laws of earlier holders.
- Secrecy is a family discipline: no photographs together, no shared events, different banks, different lawyers.

### The list (the real motive)
- The wealth was never the point. The Seats were promised that, at the end of the lease, **their families would be Taken** (see [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md), "The lease").
- Every Factor, every Steward and every family office is working, unknowingly, to keep a few hundred names on that list.
- **The tragedy:** the Taken are simply the easiest to harvest. The Lodge has spent sixty years making sure its children are first.
- **The Lodge isn't the first Table** (see [deep time](deep-time.md)). Earlier circles were promised the same thing. Nobody knows what became of them.

### Running the campaign through subordinates
The Seats themselves almost never act. Orders pass down three layers, each knowing less:

| Layer | Who | Knows about the Tenants? |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** | The twelve | Yes ("Weather ✓") |
| **Factors** | The heads of each family office: lawyers, bankers, fund managers who run the money and pass on instructions | No. They think they serve an eccentric, very successful family. |
| **Stewards** | Fixers, analysts, account operators, drivers (Cobb, Len's "cash man", the @longweather_ team) | No ("Weather ✗"). They do tasks without seeing the design. |

## Structure

| Role | Count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Founders** | 3 (now all dead) | Original contactees; wrote the Minutes |
| **Seats** | 12 (+1 empty) | Voting members; each numbered chair carries a fixed Portfolio and passes to the next person seated. Seat Thirteen is the Tenants' and is never sat in. |
| **Factors** | 12 offices | Run each Seat's family office and pass instructions down. Don't know about the Tenants. |
| **Stewards** | a few hundred worldwide | Unwitting or semi-witting helpers who carry out tasks without knowing the whole design |
| **The Listener** | 1 | The only member permitted to receive Tenant counsel directly; the role passes on death |

### Portfolios
Each Seat holds a broad area of slow influence: **Faith**, **Assembly** (organised labour), **Ledger** (money and prices), **Hearth** (family and housing), **Signal** (media and attention), **Engine** (technology), **Border** (movement of people), and others. The full seat-by-seat table is in [canon decisions D1](canon-decisions.md).

**Writing note:** the **Border** Portfolio's job in canon is to *manufacture conflict about* migration through panic, rumour and blame. Migrants themselves have no part in the plan. They are among its targets, like everyone else. The Lodge pushes every side of every quarrel at once, because the quarrel is the point.

## Methods

The Lodge almost never acts directly. Its tools are:

1. **Nudges.** Tiny, deniable changes: a funding priority, a delayed report, a rumour placed at the right dinner party.
2. **Pressure tides.** Small stresses timed to land together so that the strain compounds.
3. **Decoys.** Every crisis is paired with a convenient villain for the public to blame. *The Lodge wants you angry at your neighbour.*
4. **Silence.** No Seat has ever been photographed with another.

## Internal fractures (story engines)

- **The Accountants and the Gardeners.** The Accountants believe the Tenants' plan is benevolent stewardship. The Gardeners have begun to suspect that "pruning" means something they never agreed to.
- **The 1987 Walkout.** Seat Seven left the Table, then died in an unexplained "boating accident". Seat Seven's papers are the source of the Archivist's documents.
- **The succession problem.** The current Listener is old, and nobody knows who the Tenants will choose next.
- **Seat Eleven (Commons)** is the present-day Gardener: quietly recording doubts since Mother Quill's "let her be the one who explains it". In the book (ch 27) Seat Eleven quotes Marguerite and leaves, **the first Seat lost in thirty-nine years**.

## Symbols & tells (for ARG puzzles)

- **The empty thirteenth chair**, which appears in sketches and doodles in the documents. It is the Tenants' place, not a vacancy.
- **The phrase "for the long weather"**, used as a sign-off.
- **Harrowgate grey (#6B6E70)**, used on letterheads.
- **Dates written as day-count since 14 Oct 1962** (e.g. "Day 22,640").

## Hard-rule reminders

- No Seat is ever modelled on, named after, or hinted to be a real person.
- No real institution is described as a Lodge front. Invented fronts only: **the Harlow-Vane Institute for Systems Studies**, **Larkspire Mutual Re**, **the Ashby Trust**.
- **No real company is ever shown as Lodge-owned, Lodge-funded or Lodge-influenced.** The Table's holdings are in **fictional** global companies only (Pellucid, Tollgate, Halvard Dynamics, Nimfold, Lanternwork). Real companies such as Apple, Amazon, Tesla or any real AI lab must never be named or clearly imitated as Table investments.

# The Tenants

> "They do not want the house. They want the people in it to stop moving the furniture."
> (Vesper, Post #19)

## Summary

The Tenants are a non-human intelligence. Their nature is **never confirmed on the page**, and that ambiguity is a core rule of the series. They have no ships, no invasion and no bodies anyone has seen. They influence humanity entirely through **counsel** given to a handful of people, and through patience measured in generations.

## What the audience is allowed to know

| Layer | Revealed in | What's learned |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ARG Phase 1–2 | "Something" advised a group in 1962. Could be a hoax, a cult, a madness. |
| 2 | ARG Phase 3 / audio S1 | Contact happened through machines (teletype, later telephone exchanges, later data centres). |
| 3 | Book, Act 2 | The Tenants experience time differently. They plan in centuries. |
| 4 | Book, Act 3 | Their goal: a **smaller, stiller** humanity. They are **tenant farmers**: the harvest, the inventory, the Taken, the cull, and the earlier Tables (see "The lease" and [deep time](deep-time.md)). |
| 5 | Book 1, ch 26 | **The Tenants are real, and fully explained:** the managing agents of Earth, tenant farmers who answer to a landlord nobody has met. |
| 6 | Book 1 epilogue, then Book 2 | **The Freeholder.** The notice of inspection arrives. The landlord becomes Book 2's threat (see [the ladder](the-ladder.md)). |

## How they communicate

- **Through infrastructure, never in person.** Teletype (1962), telephone exchange crosstalk (1970s–80s), fax noise (1990s), and now **anomalous output in automated systems**.
- **In counsel, not commands.** Their messages are gentle, reasonable and plausible. That is what makes them frightening.
- **The "Long Weather" register.** They talk about societies the way a farmer talks about weather and soil: *drought, pruning, fallow seasons, thinning the rows*.

### Sample counsel (canon fragments from the Harrowgate Minutes)
- *"A field that is crowded fights itself. Let it."*
- *"Do not take their gods. Let the gods grow quiet."*
- *"When the hands are no longer needed, the hands will be fewer."*
- *"Give every fear a face that is not ours."*

## The plan, as the Tenants frame it

1. **Unmoor**: loosen shared anchors such as faith, party and place.
2. **Unbind**: dissolve collective leverage (unions, guilds, community institutions).
3. **Unsettle**: keep populations under constant low-level economic strain.
4. **Divide**: make sure every hardship has a human scapegoat.
5. **Replace**: let machines make mass labour unnecessary.
6. **Thin**: let numbers fall on their own, through declining birth rates, despair and attrition. **Never through any visible act.**

**Writing rule:** the "thinning" is always slow, impersonal and deniable. Never depict it as targeting any real-world group, and never suggest real public-health measures, medicines or vaccines are part of it. **The cull** (below) exists **only in the book and film**, and its method is **never shown** (canon decision D11).

## The lease (writers' room canon; revealed in Act 3 and the film)

**The arrangement.** The Tenants don't own Earth. They **lease** it from **the Freeholder**, something vast and never seen, and they work it as **tenant farmers**:
- **The field** is Earth. **The crop** is humanity: what we make (technologies, resources, intelligence) and **what we are** (minds, habits, voices).
- **The farmhands** are the Lodge, paid in kind, which is why they got rich.
- **The rent** has two parts: **a share of the harvest**, and **the inventory**, a complete, ordered record of the field and everything in it.
- **Developers, not just overseers:** since 1962 the counsel has steered humanity towards containers, computers, networks, phones and AI, because **that's the crop**. Every Lodge investment (Pellucid, Tollgate, Halvard, Nimfold, Lanternwork) is the Tenants developing the farm.
- **The AI race is the rent collection.** Devices capture, the cloud stores, and models compress us into something that can be handed over. *LET THEM RACE*: the faster they race, the sooner the inventory is complete.

**Keeping the field alive.** A farmer who exhausts the soil early gets nothing. So the Tenants keep Earth **sustainable until the harvest**: frightened but never burned (*IT WILL NOT BURN*, 1962), stretched but never broken.

**The Taken.** Before the end of the lease, the **brightest and most attractive** are taken and the rest are left to the thinning.
- **"Attractive" means attractive to the harvest**: whoever draws the most attention, data and influence. It never means physical appearance, and the selection is **never** by race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, gender, disability or any real group (canon decision D12).
- **What "taken" means is never confirmed on the page:** copied whole into the inventory, or carried off when the lease ends. *Writers' room only (D16):* their complete records are kept back as **seed** and used to plant households in new fields on the Freeholder's estate; the originals stay behind. Never revealed, only hinted (*KEPT BACK*, ch 25–26). The seed is a record, never a body.
- **The Lodge's real motive:** its members were promised their families would be Taken. The list, not the money, is why nobodies served for sixty years.
- **The twist:** the "best" are simply **the easiest to harvest**, the most legible and most fully captured. To the Tenants, being Taken means being finished. The people they *can't* inventory are ordinary, stubborn people who choose each other.

**The cull (book and film only).** At the end of the lease, the Tenants mean to **clear the field**: keep the harvest and reduce the occupants to whatever makes the house easy to hand back. "Fewer" was always a **date**. Crane's Model 7 was forecasting it. **Its method is never shown and never linked to any real-world mechanism** (canon decision D11).

**Why they're afraid.** They've been evicted before. They're tenants too, and an **inspection** is coming. A loud, crowded, ungovernable household fails.

**The hidden engine (writers' room only, never revealed):** the currency is the count; only the bottom rung makes anything; the inventory is the check-out inventory; a lease ends with the household handed on or cleared; the Taken are seed; the yield goes to the Freeholder's estate, for accumulation and nothing more. See canon decision D16.

**What *INTERESTING* means (ch 30).** The Tenants see something that isn't on any inventory and can't be harvested: tenants on every rung refusing an inspection together. Either it threatens the lease, or it's the first thing on this farm worth keeping. The cull is **postponed, not cancelled**.

**How far back it goes:** see [deep time](deep-time.md). **How it's structured:** see [the ladder](the-ladder.md).

## Why? (open theories, for fan speculation)

Seed these in the ARG and let the community argue:
- **The Gardener theory:** humanity is a crop, and the Tenants are managing yield. *(Closest to the truth. Let fans get there.)*
- **The Quarantine theory:** a large, loud humanity would attract something worse.
- **The Inheritance theory:** they are preparing Earth for someone else.
- **The Mirror theory:** there are no Tenants. The Lodge invented them so they would never have to own their choices. *(Fans may debate it during the ARG. **Retired in Book 1, ch 26**, which confirms the Tenants are real. The doubt moves up a rung: is there really a Freeholder?)*

- A **three-tone chime** (G–E♭–G) heard in old recordings of the contact.
- Text from the Tenants **never uses the word "I"**.
- Errors in automated systems that form **12×12 grids**.

# Deep time: the tenancy of Earth

How long the Tenants have held the lease, and what the arrangement has been. This is the history the story reveals one layer at a time (see [the ladder](the-ladder.md)).

## Guardrails (read first)

1. **No "ancient aliens".** The Tenants built nothing. No pyramid, temple, monument, technology or scripture of any real culture is credited to them. **Humans built everything. The Tenants only kept the books.**
2. **No real collapse, war, famine, plague or fall of a civilisation** is presented as the Tenants' doing (rule 4). Characters may wonder; the story never confirms.
3. **No real orders, societies or religions** are earlier "Tables". Freemasons, Templars, the "Illuminati", religious orders and similar are never used. Predecessors are **invented and anonymous**.
4. **Real history appears only as accurately described backdrop**, never as evidence.
5. **Prehistoric deep time (before humans) is fair game for SF**, because no real people are implicated.

---

## 1. Before us: earlier households

Earth has been let before. Long before humans, other households held the field, and some lost it.

- **The great extinctions** in the fossil record were **failed inspections**: households that grew too loud, too crowded, too destructive, and lost the lease. The Freeholder doesn't evict gently.
- The Tenants weren't here for most of these. They know them the way a new tenant knows the previous occupants: from marks on the walls.
- **Canon line (Fragment 9, book):** *THE HOUSE HAS BEEN EMPTY BEFORE. IT WAS QUIETER.*

## 2. The lease begins

- The Tenants took the lease on Earth **long before written history**, when the field was "not yet planted": humans were few, scattered, and not yet a crop.
- They're **tenant farmers** (see [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md), "The lease"). They develop the field to raise its yield, keep a share, and pay rent to the Freeholder in **harvest** and **inventory**.
- To them the human story is a **planting season**. Settlements, surpluses, cities, trade and machines were all yield. They didn't cause any of it. They farmed it.

## 3. The counters

Across every era the Tenants have spoken to the same kind of person: **those who count**. Scribes, granary clerks, census-takers, tax assessors, harbour-masters, actuaries, statisticians. They were overlooked, competent and *not important*. The 1962 demographer was the latest in a very long line.

- **Why counters:** the rent includes an **inventory**, and inventories are kept by clerks. On Day 0 the machine told THREE: *THE ONE WHO COUNTS WILL KEEP THE BOOK.* (ch 1)
- **What the counters got:** what the Lodge gets. Foresight, then wealth, then a promise that their families would be **Taken**.
- **What became of them:** they were never told. See §5.

## 4. "The first writing was a receipt" (real backdrop)

Some of the earliest writing we know of was **accounting**: tallies of grain, livestock and labour, pressed into clay. That's a real historical fact, described accurately.

In the story, Nell notices it (ch 12): *"The first thing anyone ever wrote down was who owed what."*
- **The story never claims the Tenants caused writing.** It lets the rhyme sit: the inventory has been kept for a very long time, and people have always written it down for someone else.

## 5. The earlier Tables

The Lodge isn't the first circle of letting agents.

- In the **Ashby store safe**, beside copies 1 and 2 of the Harrowgate Minutes, is **the older ledger**: a bound book in a dead script, dated by a **different day-count** that began long before 1962. It records counsel in the same farming language, with the same absence of "I" (see the [artefacts register](artefacts-register.md)).
- **The question it raises (ch 26):** *"We weren't the first Table."* What happened to the last one?
- **The implied answer, never confirmed:** each earlier Table was retired when its season ended. Its counters were promised to be Taken. Whether they were, nobody knows.
- **Writers' room only (D16):** they were. Their records were kept back as seed for other fields, and they themselves stayed behind. In ch 26 the older ledger's names are set out like a stock list under *KEPT BACK*; nothing more is ever shown.
- **Writers' room only:** there have been at least four earlier Tables in recorded history. None is ever identified with a real organisation, place or people.

## 6. Every age's revolt

- In every era, somewhere, the people at the bottom of the ladder **looked sideways**: they stopped blaming each other and refused the rent together.
- The book alludes to this **only in general terms** ("every age has had its peasants' revolt"). It never names or retells a specific real uprising, riot or revolution, and never attributes any to the Tenants.
- **Why the Tenants have never finished the inventory:** sideways connection is the one yield that can't be written down. Every revolt left a gap in the ledger.

## 7. Now: the last season

*(For what the yield is for and where it goes, see canon decision D16, writers' room only.)*

- Machines that can count everything, devices in every pocket and models trained on everything people say mean that, for the first time, **the inventory can be completed**.
- That's why the counsel quickened after 1962, why the Table funds the AI race (*LET THEM RACE*), and why the end of this lease feels close: the harvest, the cull (book and film only; see canon decision D11), and the Taken.
- **The hope:** what happens in chapters 28–30. Tenants on every rung refuse the inspection at once.

# The ladder

The book's deep structure: **the same shape at every scale.** Each level is a tenant of the one above, can't see past it, and passes the pressure down.

| Level | Above | Below | What flows up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cosmic | The Freeholder | **The Tenants** | Rent: the harvest and the inventory |
| Planetary | The Tenants | **The Lodge** (the Table) | Obedience, the work, the list |
| Human power | The Seats | **Factors, then Stewards** | Instructions, silence |
| Everyday life | Larkspire, Coyle's, the platforms | **Nell, Danny, Jack** | Rent, labour, attention |

## Rules at every level

- **Nobody sees two rungs up.** Stewards don't know about the Seats, the Seats have never met the Tenants, and the Tenants have never seen the Freeholder.
- **Nobody says "I".** Every level speaks in the passive voice: "per instr.", "placement noted", "it has been agreed", *THERE IS TIME*.
- **Everyone is promised a place higher up.** Stewards get cash, Seats get the list, the Tenants hope to keep the lease.
- **Everyone lives on the rung below.** Only the bottom rung makes anything; every rung above lives on what flows up, and puts it into owning more (homes, stakes, worlds). Writers' room only: see canon decision D16.
- **Everyone fears an inspection.** Nell's 3:40 a.m. text from Larkspire and the Freeholder's visit are the same event at different scales.

## The reveal, one rung per stage

1. **ARG / Act 1:** a firm moved machines off the books (Coyle's).
2. **Act 1–2:** a secret circle is behind the firm (the Lodge).
3. **Act 2:** something non-human is behind the circle (the Tenants).
4. **Act 3:** even that is renting, and harvesting to pay rent ([the lease](the-tenants.md), [deep time](deep-time.md)).
5. **Book 2:** the Freeholder becomes the threat. It's still unseen (renters never meet the landlord), known only through its paperwork, and nobody knows whether it exists at all.

## The key: sideways

- The ladder is **vertical**: power, rent, instructions and fear all run up and down.
- The only thing it can't model, inventory or harvest is **horizontal**: people on the same rung choosing each other.
- **The Wall** (ch 18): every thread runs sideways. At first that's the horror, because nothing points up. In the end it's the hope.
- **The ending** (ch 28–30): tenants on every rung refuse the inspection together.

**One line:** *Every ladder goes up. Nobody ever looked sideways.*

# Canon decisions

Answers to the canon questions raised while drafting Phase 1–2. Each entry gives the decision, the reasoning, and **what the audience learns, and when**. Writers' room only: nothing here is published as-is.

---

## D1. Seat numbering, and who "Four" is

**Decision.** The Table has **thirteen numbered chairs**. A Seat is a chair, not a person: when a holder dies or leaves, the next person seated takes the same number and Portfolio. The Founders held Seats One to Three.

| Seat | Portfolio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One | **Faith** | Founder One's seat (Founder One d. 1976) |
| Two | **Assembly** (organised labour) | Founder Two's seat (d. 2001) |
| Three | **Signal** (media and attention) | Founder Three's seat (d. 2001). She noticed the counsel never says "I". |
| Four | **Ledger** (money and prices) | Current holder seated 1979, the chair's second holder (the first was one of the nine seated by 1971); the active antagonist |
| Five | **Engine** (technology) | Oversaw the move from teletype to exchange to data centre |
| Six | **Border** (manufacturing conflict *about* migration) | Decoy work only. Migrants are never part of the plan. |
| Seven | **Hearth** (family, housing) | Marguerite Aldane's seat, empty since 1987 |
| Eight | **Ground** (land and food) | |
| Nine | **Ledger-Minor** (debt and credit) | Seat Four's junior |
| Ten | **Archive** (history, memory, what gets remembered) | |
| Eleven | **Commons** (public spaces, clubs, institutions that bring people together) | |
| Twelve | **Ward** (security, secrecy, Stewards) | Commands Cobb's network |
| **Thirteen** | **None. It's the Tenants' chair.** | Always empty. The "empty thirteenth chair" was never a vacancy. It's the guest's place at the table. |

"Four" in Marguerite's 1987 note is therefore **the current Seat Four, the Ledger**, the same man who sends Cobb after Vesper in 2026. He has believed in "gardening" for nearly fifty years.

**Why:** the chairs carry history across generations, and the empty chair becomes the Lodge's most chilling symbol.

**Audience learns:** that Seats are inherited (book Act 1); that Seat Thirteen belongs to the Tenants (book Act 2, foreshadowed by the crossed-out chair).

**Consequence for "Thirteen chairs. Never more.":** twelve people, one empty chair. Marguerite counted the guest.

---

## D2. Who "T." is

**Decision.** "**H.H. liaison (with T.)**" means Crane was the **human recorder at contact sessions** from 1979 to 1987: he sat with the apparatus, logged its output, and ran the numbers the counsel implied. "T." is the Tenants. Lodge paperwork never writes the full word.

**Why:** it explains why the Lodge kept Crane close after 2009 (he has *heard* them), and why his remorse is specific, not general.

**Audience learns:** the hint is in the card 0144 alt text (Phase 2). Crane confirms it in person in the audio series.

---

## D3. What happened to the teletype

**Decision.**
- **1962–71:** the teletype at Harrowgate House is the only channel.
- **3 Mar 1971:** removed to the **Ashby store**, a bonded warehouse the Ashby Trust owns (see [locations](locations-and-organisations.md)). Counsel moves to **telephone exchange crosstalk** after that.
- **1983:** the Lodge commissions a dedicated exchange cabinet, nicknamed **"the Exchange"**, installed in the Listening Room at Harrowgate. This is the wardrobe-sized thing Len moved, the one that "hummed with the power off". After the 1989 fire it goes to the Ashby store and runs there until fax lines replace it in 1994.
- **Today:** the teletype and the Exchange both sit, unplugged, in the Ashby store. **In book Act 3 the teletype prints again**, unplugged, for the first time since 1971.

**Why:** two machines give two different payoffs (Len's memory and the climax), and the Ashby store becomes a key location for the book and the film.

---

## D4. Who runs @longweather_

**Decision.** @longweather_ belongs to **Seat Three (Signal)**, working with Seat Six (Border). It's run by a small Steward team in a rented office, and its posts are drafted by a text-generation system the Engine Seat trains on decades of Lodge "decoy" briefings.

**The tell:** in four years of posts, **@longweather_ has never once used the word "I"**, exactly like the Tenants' counsel. Vesper spots it in Phase 4. It's the hard evidence that the account is the Lodge, and it raises an uncomfortable question about who is actually writing.

Full detail: [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md) ("The lease"), [deep-time.md](deep-time.md), [the-ladder.md](the-ladder.md).

*(This replaces the earlier "Quarantine" motive.)*

*Fixes v1.3 (idea #6), accepted by Martin 28 Oct 2026.* Three ordinary deaths are spun into a rumour that "someone is killing people like us". ## D15. The line: Cobb's reports

*Martin, 28 Oct 2026.* Cobb reports into a telephone line that answers in noise. It's a thread of suspense and wonder through Acts Two and Three, and it ends with the line reaching Nell in the epilogue. The Freeholder stays out of it.

**Principle: easy to understand, hard to fathom.** Every beat is clear on the page: where Cobb is, what he says, what Nell hears. What it *means* stays just out of reach until ch 23, and the epilogue reopens it. Each appearance must thrill or unsettle; none explains.

- **The habit.** Once a week, at **02:10** (the machine's hour, though Cobb has never been told why), Cobb rings a number from **the old phone box on Nell's road**. It's one of the last kiosks with a working phone; the others nearby were turned into a book swap and a defibrillator cabinet (a real trend, as backdrop). He has done it for years (length fixed in drafting; estimate: about twenty). The day of the week is fixed in drafting.

The other end is hiss with the rhythm of speech and no words, sometimes with something like the three notes underneath. ## D16. The engine of the lease (writers' room only; never revealed)

*Martin, 29 Oct 2026.* What the Tenants and the Freeholder want, where the yield goes, and what happens to the Taken. This is the **hidden engine** behind D10. The books never state it. It works in the background, so every hint points the same way. D10's reveal order is unchanged: Act 3 reveals the harvest, the Taken, the cull and what the Tenants are; nothing below is ever explained on the page.

**The model**
- **The currency is the count.** On the Tenants' and the Freeholder's rungs, value is how completely and quietly something has been counted. Ordered, recorded and finished is valuable; loud, tangled and uncountable is worthless or damaging. Money is the human rung's small copy of it ("the first writing was a receipt", ch 12). The lease's other measure is **time**: the term, the long weather.
- **Only the bottom rung makes anything.** Humanity produces; every rung above lives on what flows up. The Tenants live on the counted **surplus** (what a household makes beyond what it needs to live: labour, invention, ideas, attention) and pay rent from it. The Freeholder lives on the rent.
- **The Tenants keep the house and farm the household.** Earth is the Freeholder's property; the Tenants keep it fit to hand back. Humanity is the crop. "Balance" means enough people to yield, few enough not to damage the house, quiet enough to count.
- **The inventory is the check-out inventory.** As in a British letting, it records the house and its contents so the landlord can judge the condition at inspection. A complete inventory is the Tenants' defence; an incomplete one loses the lease. The AI race is the first tool that can complete it before the inspection date (*LET THEM RACE*).
- **Two ways a lease ends.** (1) **The house passes:** the Freeholder lets it again with the household in it, like a sale with sitting tenants; humanity passes to new farmers, smaller, quieter and counted. That is what the cull is for. (2) **The house fails:** it's cleared, the household ends (the fossil record's extinctions, D13 deep time), and the Tenants lose the lease. Neither ending is freedom.
- **The Taken are seed.** A Taken person is a finished entry: fully counted, the easiest to harvest. Their **complete records** are kept back, as a farmer keeps back the best of the crop, and used to plant households in **new fields** on the Freeholder's estate. The originals stay in the field with everyone else. The Lodge's promise ("your families will be Taken") comes true in the cruellest way: copies of them are raised elsewhere as someone else's crop. Earlier Tables' counters were promised the same.
- **Where the yield goes: the estate.** Ideas and inventions go up the ladder; the Taken plant new fields; the rent keeps the estate and takes on more houses. **The purpose is accumulation and nothing more.** Larkspire buys homes, the Lodge buys stakes, the Freeholder takes on worlds.
- **What can't be harvested** is effort spent sideways, between people on the same rung (a knock, a spare key, a rota, a strike). It never becomes surplus, never reaches the inventory, and can't be copied. That's why the strike frightens the Tenants, and why no Taken copy carries it.

**Never revealed.** No character learns or states any of this, and no narration explains it, in Book 1, Book 2, the film or the audio drama. **The ARG never hints at it at all.** It may only be felt through hints like these, each deniable on its own:
- **The list's heading (ch 25):** the Seats' list is headed *KEPT BACK*, a farmer's phrase that the Lodge reads as rescue.
- **The older ledger (ch 26):** Nell, a reconciler, sees that the earlier Table's names are set out like a stock list, under the same heading, each with a mark she can't read.
- **A line of counsel** that reads as a farming proverb: *THE BEST OF THE CROP IS NOT CONSUMED.*
- **The epilogue's notice** arrives the way post comes through an agent's office: someone holds paperwork on many houses.

**Guardrails**
- **The seed is a record, never a body.** No DNA, tissue, cloning or biological collection, and **no medical, health or care institution, real or fictional**, is ever involved or implied (rule 6, D11, D14).
- **Earth's humanity is the original.** It arose on its own and built everything; nothing was ever planted on Earth from elsewhere (D13).
- **The Tenants are never displaced migrants or refugees** in metaphor or detail (rules 1 and 9).
- **The Tenants are not environmentalists.** Their "balance" is a landlord's logic about property value, never ecology; the cull is never presented as good for the planet, and no real environmental issue is blamed on or credited to them (D13).
- **No real firm, fund, AI lab or person** keeps the inventory or profits from the estate (rule 2). It's never offered as a real theory of money, AI or economics (rule 6).
- **Selection stays by legibility only**, never by appearance or any group (D12).
