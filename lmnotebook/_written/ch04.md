**Label:** THE DEMOGRAPHER · DAY 3,061 · HARROWGATE, 2 MARCH 1971 (section label: THE ACTUARY · LE HAVRE, THE SAME NIGHT)
**Narration:** interlude, close third person. First the demographer (THREE), then the French actuary who left in 1962.
**Dates:** Tuesday 2 March 1971 (Day 3,061). Coyle's collects the machine the next day.
**Length:** about 1,450 words. Draft v1.2.

**What happens**
- The three who stayed are getting rich: better cars, cashmere, early stakes in container shipping bought through "a trust with a dull name". *They had been nobodies.* The demographer is uneasy at how easy it was.
- The Long Room now seats twelve, including a quiet New Zealand weather clerk (the future Mother Quill). A thirteenth chair is always pushed in, because on the first night the German said *no*.
- Only the three sit with the machine. The American now listens, and the German records everything (recording HG-71).
- The counsel has been moving to the telephone line. Tonight the machine prints *THE LINE IS OPEN* and *THE MACHINE IS NOT NEEDED HERE. IT WILL BE KEPT.* Asked where it goes: *NOT FAR. ONLY ELSEWHERE.*
- *The machine would not print again for fifty-five years.*
- In Le Havre the same night, the actuary plays the three tones on his mother's piano, feels a presence, and closes the lid for good. Twenty-six years later his son sells the piano, badly out of tune.
