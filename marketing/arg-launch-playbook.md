# ARG launch playbook: the 8-week pilot

**Goal:** prove there's an audience. At week 8 you should have numbers, player-made theories and a waiting list to put in front of agents and publishers, without breaking any hard rule.

---

## 1. Success measures (estimates, not benchmarks)

ARG uptake varies enormously. These are **working targets for a solo pilot**, not industry figures. Adjust them after week 2.

| Measure | Week 8 target (estimate) | Why it matters for the pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter / waiting-list sign-ups | 500–1,500 | The clearest proof of demand |
| Active community members (Discord or forum) | 100–300 | Shows engagement, not just clicks |
| Puzzles solved within their window | P1–P4 all solved in 6 hours to 3 days | Proves the design works |
| Player theories and fan-made content logged | 20+ | Shows the world is "sticky" |
| "What's your Day?" cards shared | 200+ | Proves the hook spreads |
| Incidents (someone taking it as real-world fact) | **0** | Safety, and publisher confidence |

## 4. Finding the first 100 players, honestly

ARG communities generally welcome new games **when the creator says up front that it's theirs**. Hidden-creator "I found this weird blog" posts are seen as astroturfing and get banned. They would also break rule 8.

- **Announce as the creator** in ARG communities (e.g. ARG subreddits and Discord servers; read each one's rules first): *"I've made a slow-burn ARG for a novel in progress. Rabbit hole starts here."*
- **Puzzle and SF-book communities:** the same honest framing, focused on the puzzle or the premise.
- **Short-form clips:** the manifest, the three tones, the colour-pick reveal. Fiction-labelled in the bio and caption.
- **"What's your Day?"** is the most shareable hook. Share cards carry the book's name and a link to the About page.
- **Never:** fake reviews, fake accounts boosting each other, posting into real news or crisis threads (rule 4), or tagging real companies or people.

---

## 5. Running it solo, safely

- **Comments are held for approval.** You publish what passes the [moderation table](../campaign/moderation-and-safety.md). Budget ~30 minutes a day (estimate).
- **One break-character template**, saved and ready, for anyone distressed or sincerely asking "is this real?" (from the safety playbook).
- **Pause triggers:** anyone planning to visit a real place; doxxing; press reporting it as real; any comment linking the story to real riots, vaccines or groups. When one happens, stop new posts, post the out-of-character note, and resume only once it's resolved.
- **The book-only themes** (the cull, the Taken, deep time) **don't appear in the pilot at all.**
- **Burnout guard:** if you fall behind, push the schedule a week. A slow ARG is in-genre ("the long weather does not hurry").

## 7. Turning the pilot into a pitch (week 8)

**Capture every week, in a simple log:**
- sign-ups, community size, puzzle solve times
- the best player theories, with screenshots, **with permission and no usernames**
- clips that performed well
- any incidents and how they were handled
- at week 8, the **buying-intent survey** results (response rate and % who say they'd buy)

**The week-8 pitch pack**
1. One-page overview of the book (already drafted) and the [synopsis](../book/synopsis.md).
2. **Chapters 1–3**, polished ([1](../book/chapters/01-day-zero.md), [2](../book/chapters/02-contribution-to-the-transition.md), [3](../book/chapters/03-reconciliation.md)). This is a typical UK agent submission; check each agent's guidelines.
3. **The pilot in numbers:** the §1 table with real results.
4. **Player evidence:** 3–5 theories or fan pieces showing the world works.
5. **The transmedia plan:** ARG phases, audio, comics anthology, film.
6. **The safety design**, briefly: publishers will ask how an "is this real?" campaign avoids misinformation. The hard rules are a selling point.

Send to agents first; they open doors to publishers and to audio and screen rights.

---

## 8. Risks and fallbacks

| Risk | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Low uptake by W3 | Lean harder on "What's your Day?" and clips. Reframe as a "serialised mystery" for book communities. |
| A puzzle unsolved past its window | Vesper's own nudge, then a drop-page hint. Never fake readers. |
| Someone takes it as real | Break-character template, pause triggers (§5). |
| A name clash found mid-pilot | Rename quickly (precedent: Calder → Harlow-Vane, Meridian → Larkspire, Ninefold → Nimfold). |
| Burnout | Push the schedule a week, and say so in Vesper's voice. |
| Hostile comments or brigading | Comments held for approval. Ban and log. Escalate threats. |
