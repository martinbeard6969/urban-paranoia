# The news desk

A regular scan of worldwide news that keeps *Urban Paranoia* current. It finds real developments that sit close to the story's themes, sorts them through a safety filter, and turns the useful ones into story material.

**The core rule:** real news is **backdrop and inspiration, never evidence**. The story can show characters *worrying* about what a trend means. It never claims a real event was caused by the Lodge, the Tenants, or any real person, company, government or group.

---

## 1. Cadence

| When | What | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** (during the live campaign) | Scan and sort | `news-desk/digest-YYYY-MM-DD.md` |
| **Each phase start** | Review the last 4–6 digests for big themes | Notes in the phase plan |
| **Ad hoc** | A major development lands squarely on a theme | A digest entry, held for at least the cooling-off period (§4) |

The weekly scan can run as a scheduled task that writes the digest file for human review. **Nothing from the desk is published without an editor's sign-off.**

## 3. The safety filter

Every candidate story gets one of three ratings.

### 🟢 Green: usable
- Broad trends, official statistics, published research, technology announcements and outages with no harm to people.
- It can be described accurately, with a source, without naming a real person as a villain.

### 🟡 Amber: inspiration only
- A specific real event that's useful but too close to real people or institutions to touch directly. **Fictionalise it:** invent a parallel event in the story's own world (the city, Coyle's, Larkspire, Coldharbour Data) with new names, places and details. It must not be recognisable as a retelling of a real event.
- Examples: a company's layoffs → Coyle's makes a fictional announcement; a big cloud outage → DC-3 has an invented outage with a 12×12 pattern in its logs.

### 🔴 Red: never use
- Deaths, attacks, disasters, riots, protests, crimes and court cases.
- Elections, named politicians, political parties and campaigns.
- **Immigration and asylum incidents, or any story about a specific ethnic, religious or national group.**
- Health, medicine, vaccines and public-health measures.
- Market moves, bank or currency stories that could look like financial advice.
- Anything about a private individual.
- Anything already the subject of real conspiracy theories. We don't add fuel.
- **Specific speech, hate-speech or blasphemy laws, bills, prosecutions or court cases.** (Broad, neutral surveys can be 🟢.)
- **Anything presenting multiculturalism, integration or migration as an "experiment", a plan, or a group's fault.** Only broad, neutral social-cohesion surveys may be 🟡, for writers'-room background only. See [modern pressures §5](../bible/modern-pressures.md).
- **Real social movements, causes, protests, wars and conflicts, and stories about gender identity or immigration enforcement.** Use the invented flashpoints in [modern pressures §6](../bible/modern-pressures.md) instead. Broad, neutral polarisation surveys may be 🟡.
- **Named real organisations linked to "own nothing" or "great reset" narratives.** The slogan's real origin may be stated accurately (Danny's correction), nothing more.

**When unsure, rate it amber or red.**

---

## 4. Rules for using material

1. **No causation.** Never write, imply or hint that the Lodge or the Tenants caused, planned or benefited from a real event. Characters may notice that a real trend *rhymes* with the Minutes ("it's like what they wrote in 1962"). The story never confirms the link.
2. **Accuracy.** Real facts are described correctly and attributed to their real source. No exaggerating, cropping or misquoting to make them darker.
3. **Clear seams.** A reader must always be able to tell where the real report ends and the fiction begins. In posts, Vesper links to the real source and keeps her speculation clearly marked as hers.
4. **A cooling-off period.** Wait **at least four weeks** after a story breaks before using it, even when it's green. Never use breaking news.
5. **Danny's check.** Ask "would this make a reader angrier at their neighbours?" If yes, cut it or reframe it so the story's message (every scapegoat is a decoy) is the takeaway.
6. **No real companies or people as characters.** Real organisations can be *cited* as the source of a statistic. They are never shown acting in the story.
7. **Log it.** Every use is logged in the digest with its source, rating, how it was used, and who signed it off.

## 5. Ways to use selected stories ("interactive integration")

| Format | What it is | Ratings allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **Vesper's "Weather report"** | A short recurring post: Vesper links a real statistic or study (e.g. a new birth-rate figure), describes it accurately, and says what it makes her *feel*, given what she's found. It ends with a question to readers. | 🟢 |
| **"The real backdrop"** | An out-of-world note on `/about` or in the book's afterword: the real trends behind the story, explained plainly with sources. It doubles as media literacy. | 🟢 |
| **Fictional echoes** | An invented event in the story's world that mirrors a real development, e.g. Coyle's announces more automation, or DC-3 has an outage with a 12×12 log pattern. | 🟢 🟡 |
| **Reader threads** | Vesper asks readers what *trends* they're seeing in their own work and towns (never incidents or people). Moderated under the [safety playbook](moderation-and-safety.md). | 🟢 |
| **Plot seeds** | A trend inspires a future beat in the bible (e.g. a new Portfolio storyline). It stays in the writers' room. | 🟢 🟡 |

## 5a. Commentary review (YouTube)

After the weekly scan, each **selected** story (green or amber) gets a second pass. We look at how widely followed YouTube channels discuss it, to understand the angles, arguments and public mood around it. This sharpens Vesper's voice and the readers' questions she answers, and it gives the writers' room more detail on the issue.

### Step 1: Find channels (5–10 per story)
- **Discovery:** use the official **YouTube Data API v3** (`search.list` for videos on the story's topic, then `channels.list` for subscriber counts), or search manually. Record the channel name, subscriber count, video title, URL and date.
- **Selection criteria:**
  - **Size:** large and influential, roughly 100k+ subscribers, so we hear what large audiences are hearing.
  - **Mix:** some **explainers** (journalists, economists, technologists, academics) for depth, and some **mainstream commentary** for mood. Aim for different viewpoints.
  - **Exclude:** channels whose main content is conspiracy theory, hate, or blaming ethnic, religious, national or migrant groups. We're studying the issue, not importing those narratives. Tag them as excluded in the digest.
  - **Recency:** videos within the last 3 months.

### Step 2: Get transcripts (permitted methods only)
- **Don't bulk-scrape YouTube.** Automated scraping and unofficial transcript tools break YouTube's Terms of Service, and copying whole transcripts raises copyright issues.
- **Allowed:**
  1. **YouTube's own "Show transcript" panel**, read video by video by a person (or by Claude in the browser pane under the user's direction), noting the key passages.
  2. **The official Captions API** (`captions.download`), which only works with the channel owner's permission. Use it if a creator agrees.
  3. **Creator-published transcripts**: many channels post full transcripts or show notes on their own sites or newsletters.
  4. **Asking the creator.** For a channel we rely on often, request permission directly.
- **Never** download videos or audio to transcribe them ourselves without permission.
- **If the transcript panel won't load** (seen on the first run with YouTube's newer tabbed panel), record the video's description and chapters, mark the transcript "not read", and try a creator-published transcript instead. Don't work around it with unofficial tools.
- **Check the latest version** of any study a video cites. Commentary often quotes superseded figures.

### Step 3: Analyse
For each video, record **in our own words**:
- **Main claims**, with each factual claim **checked against a primary source** (official statistics, the original study, the original report). Unverified claims are marked as such and never used as fact.
- **Angles and framings**: how it's explained and what it's compared to.
- **Audience mood**: what the top comments worry about (themes only; no usernames).
- **Questions people keep asking**: material for Vesper's reader threads.
- **Counter-arguments**: material for Danny.
- **Red-filter check**: if the discussion pulls the story towards a red topic (§3), note it and keep our use away from that angle.

### Step 4: Store and use
- **Store:** notes, timestamps, links and **short quotes only (under ~25 words, attributed)**. Never full transcripts. Notes go in the digest, under the story they relate to.
- **Use:**
  - The analysis informs the **writers' room**. It never appears in in-world content as quotes.
  - **Creators are never named, quoted, impersonated or shown as characters** in in-world content, and Vesper never links to them as "evidence".
  - Out-of-world "real backdrop" notes may recommend a reputable explainer video as further viewing, with full credit.

## 7. Who does what

- **Claude:** runs the scan, writes the digest, rates the candidates, drafts proposed uses, finds commentary channels, and analyses transcripts obtained by the permitted methods in §5a.
- **An editor (human):** confirms ratings, signs off every use, and makes final calls on amber items.
- **The moderation lead:** is told about any planned reader thread before it goes up.
