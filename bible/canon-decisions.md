# Canon decisions

Answers to the canon questions raised while drafting Phase 1–2. Each entry gives the decision, the reasoning, and **what the audience learns, and when**. Writers' room only: nothing here is published as-is.

---

## D1. Seat numbering, and who "Four" is

**Decision.** The Table has **thirteen numbered chairs**. A Seat is a chair, not a person: when a holder dies or leaves, the next person seated takes the same number and Portfolio. The Founders held Seats One to Three.

| Seat | Portfolio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One | **Faith** | Founder One's seat (Founder One d. 1976) |
| Two | **Assembly** (organised labour) | Founder Two's seat (d. 2001) |
| Three | **Signal** (media and attention) | Founder Three's seat (d. 2001). She noticed the counsel never says "I". |
| Four | **Ledger** (money and prices) | Current holder seated 1979, the chair's second holder (the first was one of the nine seated by 1971); the active antagonist |
| Five | **Engine** (technology) | Oversaw the move from teletype to exchange to data centre |
| Six | **Border** (manufacturing conflict *about* migration) | Decoy work only. Migrants are never part of the plan. |
| Seven | **Hearth** (family, housing) | Marguerite Aldane's seat, empty since 1987 |
| Eight | **Ground** (land and food) | |
| Nine | **Ledger-Minor** (debt and credit) | Seat Four's junior |
| Ten | **Archive** (history, memory, what gets remembered) | |
| Eleven | **Commons** (public spaces, clubs, institutions that bring people together) | |
| Twelve | **Ward** (security, secrecy, Stewards) | Commands Cobb's network |
| **Thirteen** | **None. It's the Tenants' chair.** | Always empty. The "empty thirteenth chair" was never a vacancy. It's the guest's place at the table. |

"Four" in Marguerite's 1987 note is therefore **the current Seat Four, the Ledger**, the same man who sends Cobb after Vesper in 2026. He has believed in "gardening" for nearly fifty years.

**Why:** the chairs carry history across generations, and the empty chair becomes the Lodge's most chilling symbol.

**Audience learns:** that Seats are inherited (book Act 1); that Seat Thirteen belongs to the Tenants (book Act 2, foreshadowed by the crossed-out chair).

**Consequence for "Thirteen chairs. Never more.":** twelve people, one empty chair. Marguerite counted the guest.

## D2. Who "T." is

**Decision.** "**H.H. liaison (with T.)**" means Crane was the **human recorder at contact sessions** from 1979 to 1987: he sat with the apparatus, logged its output, and ran the numbers the counsel implied. "T." is the Tenants. Lodge paperwork never writes the full word.

**Why:** it explains why the Lodge kept Crane close after 2009 (he has *heard* them), and why his remorse is specific, not general.

**Audience learns:** the hint is in the card 0144 alt text (Phase 2). Crane confirms it in person in the audio series.

## D3. What happened to the teletype

**Decision.**
- **1962–71:** the teletype at Harrowgate House is the only channel.
- **3 Mar 1971:** removed to the **Ashby store**, a bonded warehouse the Ashby Trust owns (see [locations](locations-and-organisations.md)). Counsel moves to **telephone exchange crosstalk** after that.
- **1983:** the Lodge commissions a dedicated exchange cabinet, nicknamed **"the Exchange"**, installed in the Listening Room at Harrowgate. This is the wardrobe-sized thing Len moved, the one that "hummed with the power off". After the 1989 fire it goes to the Ashby store and runs there until fax lines replace it in 1994.
- **Today:** the teletype and the Exchange both sit, unplugged, in the Ashby store. **In book Act 3 the teletype prints again**, unplugged, for the first time since 1971.

**Why:** two machines give two different payoffs (Len's memory and the climax), and the Ashby store becomes a key location for the book and the film.

## D4. Who runs @longweather_

**Decision.** @longweather_ belongs to **Seat Three (Signal)**, working with Seat Six (Border). It's run by a small Steward team in a rented office, and its posts are drafted by a text-generation system the Engine Seat trains on decades of Lodge "decoy" briefings.

**The tell:** in four years of posts, **@longweather_ has never once used the word "I"**, exactly like the Tenants' counsel. Vesper spots it in Phase 4. It's the hard evidence that the account is the Lodge, and it raises an uncomfortable question about who is actually writing.

**Why:** the reveal is clean and fair-play (the clue is in the bible from day one), and it ties the Decoy Doctrine to the AI theme.

**Safety note:** the account's actual blame content is never written or shown. See the [safety playbook](../campaign/moderation-and-safety.md).

## D5. How @longweather_ had the phrase first, and how it knows what Vesper reads

**Decision.**
- **"Long weather"** is an old Steward dog-whistle. The Signal team used it in the account name in 2022 because, to the Lodge, it means *"we are the people with patience"*. They never expected anyone outside to recognise it.
- **How it knows which file Vesper has opened:** the Coyle's archive storage unit uses keycard access and camera monitoring, both logged through **the same routing system** installed on Day 22,861. The 144 lb server cabinet reports every time Vesper's keycard opens the unit. The Engine Seat reads the logs.

**Audience learns:** Phase 4, when Vesper works out the keycard link. It recasts the 144 lb coincidence: the server *was* the new teletype.

## D6. Crane's allegiance

**Decision.** **Both, in turn.**
1. **Handler (Phase 2 to book Act 2).** After card 0144 is opened, Seat Four tells Crane to make contact, gain Vesper's trust and find out what the Archivist has. Crane agrees, because he's afraid and because he still half believes.
2. **Defector (book Act 3).** Crane's remorse was always real. When Seat Four orders Vesper's blog discredited, Crane gives her the **original Model 7 workbooks**, which prove the Lodge modelled population decline as a goal.
3. **Ending.** Crane survives, but his Weather clearance is revoked and he lives in fear. His last line in the book: *"I counted the wrong thing for fifty years. I counted people. I should have counted the people who stayed."*

**Why:** both readings in the ARG are true, so the audience's arguments are rewarded either way.

## D7. Marguerite's death

**Decision.** **It was a genuine accident.** She went out alone in bad weather and drowned. The Lodge didn't order it, but **it let the rumour stand**, because Stewards and Seats believing the Lodge kills deserters is useful to it.

**Ruth learns this in book Act 3**, from Crane. It devastates her, because the fear that shaped her whole life was manufactured too.

**Audience learns:** never confirmed in the ARG. The book confirms it.

## D10. The Tenants' motive (writers' room; revealed in Act 3 and the film)

**Decision.** The Tenants are **tenant farmers** leasing Earth from **the Freeholder**. They develop humanity's yield (technology, resources, intelligence, and humans themselves), keep a share, and pay rent in **harvest** and **inventory**. They keep the field sustainable until the end of the lease, then mean to take the **Taken** and **cull** the rest. They're afraid because an **inspection** is coming and they've been evicted before. Full detail: [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md) ("The lease"), [deep-time.md](deep-time.md), [the-ladder.md](the-ladder.md).

*(This replaces the earlier "Quarantine" motive.)*

**Rules:**
- **The Mirror theory is retired** (decided 25 Oct 2026). Book 1, ch 26 confirms the Tenants are real. During the ARG, before the book, fans may still debate it.
- **The doubt moves up a rung:** the **"nobody" doubt** (perhaps there is no Freeholder) must stay possible through Book 2. Renters never meet the landlord.
- **Reveal order:** ARG and Act 1 suggest only that they want quiet. Act 3 reveals the harvest, the Taken, the cull and **what the Tenants are** (ch 26: managing agents and tenant farmers). The **epilogue** delivers the Freeholder's notice. **Book 2** makes the Freeholder the threat. (The film adapts Book 1.)
- *INTERESTING* (ch 30) means the Tenants have seen something not on any inventory. The cull is postponed, not cancelled.

---

## D11. The cull: safeguard

- **Book and film only.** The ARG stays at the ambiguous "thinning" level. No in-world post, drop or account ever suggests a cull is coming in the real world.
- **Method never shown.** It's a date, a forecast in Model 7, and a fear.
- **Never linked** to vaccines, medicine, food, water, real technologies, real companies or real events (rules 4 and 6).
- **Never targeted** at any group. It's about numbers, not who people are (rules 1 and 9).

---

## D12. The Taken: safeguard

- **"Brightest and most attractive" means attractive to the harvest:** attention, data, influence, legibility. Never physical appearance.
- **Never selected** by race, ethnicity, religion, nationality, gender, disability or any real group.
- **The story condemns the selection.** The Taken aren't better people. The idea that some lives are worth keeping is the Tenants' horror and the Lodge's corruption.
- **Book and film only.** No real people or groups are ever named or implied as "chosen" or "thinned".

---

## D13. Deep time: guardrail

- **No "ancient aliens".** Humans built everything. No real monument, culture, technology or scripture is credited to the Tenants.
- **No real collapse, war, plague, famine or revolt** is presented as the Tenants' doing.
- **Earlier Tables are invented and anonymous.** Never real orders, societies or religions.
- **Real history only as accurately described backdrop** (e.g. "the first writing was a receipt").
- **The ending's revolt is invented** (the Larkspire rent strike). Real riots and uprisings are never named, retold or used as models (rules 4 and 5).

## D14. The missing knock: safeguard

*Fixes v1.3 (idea #6), accepted by Martin 28 Oct 2026.* Three ordinary deaths are spun into a rumour that "someone is killing people like us". It gives outline ch 17's "crime that never happened" its content, and runs on the ch 12 counsel *GIVE EVERY FEAR A FACE THAT IS NOT OURS*. No new Tenant line, no new major character.

- **The deaths are natural.** Three people die in Act Two (ch 13, 16, 17): a fall, a fire, a heart attack or similar. Nobody causes them. The Lodge's guilt is the **story** told about them, not the deaths.
- **What links them is missing help:** a neighbour who stopped knocking, a spare key taken back, a parcel left on a step. Before the deaths, people stop knocking because of the **general fear the feeds already stir up** (Frostline, the Hush threats of ch 13, Danny's *"Everyone's frightened"* in ch 5). The killing rumour comes **after** the deaths and feeds on them.
- **No service fails on purpose.** No company, Lodge-owned or not, withdraws help, changes a rota or causes a death. If a care-alarm or welfare-check firm is named, it's invented and goes on the name-clearance list (open question 1). Never real emergency services or the NHS, and never a hint that real health or care services fail on purpose.
- **Reported, never shown.** Nell learns of each death second-hand. There's no death scene and no point of view from a victim.
- **No group selection.** Each victim is a specific person, with one planted detail while alive. The rumour sorts them into "sides"; the book shows the sorting is false.
- **Nobody the reader suspects is a killer.** The suspects (see `characters.md`, "Missing knock") are innocent or complicit only in small, human ways.
- **The plywood-window family is never one of the reader's suspects.** Only the rumour targets them. The book directs the reader's suspicion at Nell's own circle, so the reader's guilt lands on themselves, never on a group.
- **No real events.** The rumour, its wording and the crowd must not echo any specific real rumour-led disorder or attack (hard rule 4).
- **The Tenants never order deaths.** The only counsel involved is ch 12's. The Table and Ward read it their own way (D13: humans own what they build). Ch 15's *welfare-check rates down eleven per cent* is a number nobody reacts to; nobody orders, causes or welcomes a death.
- **@longweather_ is never quoted.** Nell paraphrases it.
- **Book and film only.** No ARG post, drop or account mentions the deaths or a pattern of deaths. The ARG may echo the rumour's *tone* at most.

## D15. The line: Cobb's reports

*Martin, 28 Oct 2026.* Cobb reports into a telephone line that answers in noise. It's a thread of suspense and wonder through Acts Two and Three, and it ends with the line reaching Nell in the epilogue. The Freeholder stays out of it.

**Principle: easy to understand, hard to fathom.** Every beat is clear on the page: where Cobb is, what he says, what Nell hears. What it *means* stays just out of reach until ch 23, and the epilogue reopens it. Each appearance must thrill or unsettle; none explains.

- **The habit.** Once a week, at **02:10** (the machine's hour, though Cobb has never been told why), Cobb rings a number from **the old phone box on Nell's road**. It's one of the last kiosks with a working phone; the others nearby were turned into a book swap and a defibrillator cabinet (a real trend, as backdrop). He has done it for years (length fixed in drafting; estimate: about twenty). The day of the week is fixed in drafting.
- **Only his side is clear.** He reports in short, plain sentences, in garden words: *quieter*, *not speaking*, *thinner*. The other end is hiss with the rhythm of speech and no words, sometimes with something like the three notes underneath. When it stops, he says *"Understood,"* though nothing in it could be understood.
- **He reports. He never receives counsel.** Only the Listener hears the Tenants directly (`the-lodge.md`). Cobb believes he's ringing someone in Seat Twelve's (Ward's) office on a bad line. He has never heard a word back.
- **Where it goes (ch 23).** Nobody picks up. The number routes to the racks Larkspire rents, to the system behind the 144 lb server: the one that drafts @longweather_ and never says "I". A machine has been taking his reports for years.
- **What it becomes.** His sentences surface, unattributed, in the Factors' briefing (ch 15) and, in ch 26, in the cells of Teo's 12×12 grid: the Tenants' **inventory**, the record of the field they pay rent in. A man's plain words, counted in rows.
- **The ladder in one man.** Cobb is a Larkspire tenant (ch 29). For years he has been counting his own rung for the people above it. His signature on the strike list is the moment he stops.

**On the page: two calls, one silence, one echo**

| Ch | Beat | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | **Call 1.** At 02:10, mid-way through her night with the fragments, Nell sees the phone box lit and the man from the bus stop inside it. She goes down in her socks and stands behind the box in the dark. His side: *"Wharf Road's quieter. Two more households not speaking… She's reading. Tonight. All of it… Yes. Thinner."* Hiss. *"Understood."* He walks away without looking back. She goes back up and reads until four. | Fast |
| 15 | **The echo upstairs.** One line in the Factor's briefing, among the figures, in Cobb's words: *Wharf Road quieter.* The reader recognises it; nobody at the Table does. | Beat |
| 22 | **Call 2.** Same box, same hour, and this time she's waiting, phone recording in her pocket. He reports her readers leaving and the hate in her inbox. Mid-report he stops for three seconds, the first time he has hesitated, then: *"She's still there."* | Fast |
| 23 | **The answer.** Teo runs her recording. The hiss carries the same signature as the Larkspire racks. Nobody was on the line. | Reveal |
| 26 | **The inventory.** In the 12×12 grid, a row for her street. The cells hold words, not numbers: *quieter*, *thinner*, *still there*. | Wonder |
| 29 | **The silence.** On his night, at 02:10, the box is lit and the phone in it **rings**. Nobody answers. It rings until it stops. The next day Nell finds his name on the strike list. | Still, then a jolt |
| Epilogue | **The line reaches Nell.** At 02:10 her phone rings. For about a second, before Teo speaks, there's the hiss with the rhythm of speech. Then Teo: *"It's printing."* She doesn't mention it. Unexplained: whatever Cobb reported to now has her number. | Jolt |

**Guardrails**
- **Mood and division only.** He reports on quiet, silence between neighbours, Nell's readers. Never deaths, numbers or methods, and **never the D14 streets or victims**. Ch 15's *welfare-check rates* line is Ward's, not Cobb's, and stays separate. Call 1 (ch 12) comes before the first death; call 2 (ch 22) comes after ch 20 has cleared him as "the only one who knocked".
- **The voice never forms words**, in the book, the epilogue or any adaptation. The teletype's *I* stays the only "I".
- **No real phone network, company or emergency line** is named or imitated. The number is never printed.
- **No real-world call to action:** nothing invites readers to visit, ring or watch real phone boxes (rule 5).
- **Two calls only.** A third would make it a routine.
- **Book and audio drama.** A Phase 3 audio leak of call 1 (one side clear, the other pure texture) may be proposed later, with the fiction trail (rules 7–8). Nothing in Phase 1–2.

## D16. The engine of the lease (writers' room only; never revealed)

*Martin, 29 Oct 2026.* What the Tenants and the Freeholder want, where the yield goes, and what happens to the Taken. This is the **hidden engine** behind D10. The books never state it. It works in the background, so every hint points the same way. D10's reveal order is unchanged: Act 3 reveals the harvest, the Taken, the cull and what the Tenants are; nothing below is ever explained on the page.

**The model**
- **The currency is the count.** On the Tenants' and the Freeholder's rungs, value is how completely and quietly something has been counted. Ordered, recorded and finished is valuable; loud, tangled and uncountable is worthless or damaging. Money is the human rung's small copy of it ("the first writing was a receipt", ch 12). The lease's other measure is **time**: the term, the long weather.
- **Only the bottom rung makes anything.** Humanity produces; every rung above lives on what flows up. The Tenants live on the counted **surplus** (what a household makes beyond what it needs to live: labour, invention, ideas, attention) and pay rent from it. The Freeholder lives on the rent.
- **The Tenants keep the house and farm the household.** Earth is the Freeholder's property; the Tenants keep it fit to hand back. Humanity is the crop. "Balance" means enough people to yield, few enough not to damage the house, quiet enough to count.
- **The inventory is the check-out inventory.** As in a British letting, it records the house and its contents so the landlord can judge the condition at inspection. A complete inventory is the Tenants' defence; an incomplete one loses the lease. The AI race is the first tool that can complete it before the inspection date (*LET THEM RACE*).
- **Two ways a lease ends.** (1) **The house passes:** the Freeholder lets it again with the household in it, like a sale with sitting tenants; humanity passes to new farmers, smaller, quieter and counted. That is what the cull is for. (2) **The house fails:** it's cleared, the household ends (the fossil record's extinctions, D13 deep time), and the Tenants lose the lease. Neither ending is freedom.
- **The Taken are seed.** A Taken person is a finished entry: fully counted, the easiest to harvest. Their **complete records** are kept back, as a farmer keeps back the best of the crop, and used to plant households in **new fields** on the Freeholder's estate. The originals stay in the field with everyone else. The Lodge's promise ("your families will be Taken") comes true in the cruellest way: copies of them are raised elsewhere as someone else's crop. Earlier Tables' counters were promised the same.
- **Where the yield goes: the estate.** Ideas and inventions go up the ladder; the Taken plant new fields; the rent keeps the estate and takes on more houses. **The purpose is accumulation and nothing more.** Larkspire buys homes, the Lodge buys stakes, the Freeholder takes on worlds.
- **What can't be harvested** is effort spent sideways, between people on the same rung (a knock, a spare key, a rota, a strike). It never becomes surplus, never reaches the inventory, and can't be copied. That's why the strike frightens the Tenants, and why no Taken copy carries it.

**Never revealed.** No character learns or states any of this, and no narration explains it, in Book 1, Book 2, the film or the audio drama. **The ARG never hints at it at all.** It may only be felt through hints like these, each deniable on its own:
- **The list's heading (ch 25):** the Seats' list is headed *KEPT BACK*, a farmer's phrase that the Lodge reads as rescue.
- **The older ledger (ch 26):** Nell, a reconciler, sees that the earlier Table's names are set out like a stock list, under the same heading, each with a mark she can't read.
- **A line of counsel** that reads as a farming proverb: *THE BEST OF THE CROP IS NOT CONSUMED.*
- **The epilogue's notice** arrives the way post comes through an agent's office: someone holds paperwork on many houses.

**Guardrails**
- **The seed is a record, never a body.** No DNA, tissue, cloning or biological collection, and **no medical, health or care institution, real or fictional**, is ever involved or implied (rule 6, D11, D14).
- **Earth's humanity is the original.** It arose on its own and built everything; nothing was ever planted on Earth from elsewhere (D13).
- **The Tenants are never displaced migrants or refugees** in metaphor or detail (rules 1 and 9).
- **The Tenants are not environmentalists.** Their "balance" is a landlord's logic about property value, never ecology; the cull is never presented as good for the planet, and no real environmental issue is blamed on or credited to them (D13).
- **No real firm, fund, AI lab or person** keeps the inventory or profits from the estate (rule 2). It's never offered as a real theory of money, AI or economics (rule 6).
- **Selection stays by legibility only**, never by appearance or any group (D12).
