# The Lodge

> "We were not chosen because we were the most powerful. We were chosen because we were the most willing."
> (attributed to "Founder Three", from the Harrowgate Minutes, 1963)

## Summary

The Lodge is a small, self-perpetuating, **multinational** circle of human intermediaries who have carried out the Tenants' advice since 1962. **Before contact they were nobodies:** competent, overlooked, mid-level officials. Sixty years of counsel about "which way the weather will turn" have made them, and their families, among the richest and most hidden people alive. It is **not** a government, a bank, a religion, an ethnic group or a political party. The Table has **thirteen chairs** but has never had more than **twelve seated members**: the thirteenth chair belongs to the Tenants (see [canon decisions D1](canon-decisions.md)). Members are drawn from no single nation, class or faith, and the Lodge deliberately avoids any identifying pattern so that it can never be traced.

Members never call it "the Lodge" themselves. Internally it is **"the Table"**. "The Lodge" is the outsider name Vesper picks up from a misfiled 1970s memo.

## Origins

- **Autumn 1962. The Harrowgate Contact.** The Harlow-Vane Institute hosts an invitation-only international seminar in "systems forecasting" at **Harrowgate House**, a fictional country estate in a fogbound valley. Its seven guests are **promising mid-level officials from seven countries**: clever enough to be invited, junior enough not to be missed. A Tenant makes contact through the house's experimental teletype (see [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md)). Four guests leave and remember nothing coherent. Three stay. They become the first three Founders.

**Rule:** the guests' real employers are never named. Their jobs stay generic, and none is modelled on a real person.
- **1963. The Harrowgate Minutes.** The Founders write a 40-page record of the Tenants' "counsel". Only fragments survive in-world. They are the most important artefacts in the ARG.
- **1964–1969. Seating the Table.** The Founders recruit nine more members over five years, all mid-level people (a shipping-line manager, a broadcasting engineer, a housing official, a junior diplomat, a pensions administrator and others) from Europe, the Americas, Asia, Africa and Oceania. Each one is approached at a point of personal crisis. Recruitment always takes the form of an *offer of certainty*.

## Then and now: how the Table got rich

The Tenants never told the Table to get rich. They told it **what was coming**: containers, computers, networks, phones, the move to buying everything online, batteries, machines that think. People who know which way the weather will turn, sixty years early, don't stay mid-level for long.

- **1960s–70s:** small, early stakes in container shipping, telecoms and the first personal-computer firms, through the Ashby Trust and nominee accounts.
- **1980s–2000s:** **Larkspire Mutual Re** becomes the main vehicle. Each Seat's family sets up a **family office**.
- **2000s–now:** quiet, minority stakes, never controlling, never public, in the fictional global companies that shape daily life. See [locations & organisations](locations-and-organisations.md): **Pellucid** (devices), **Tollgate** (online marketplace and cloud), **Halvard Dynamics** (electric vehicles, batteries, satellites), and **both** of the leading AI labs, **Nimfold** and **Lanternwork**. The Table funds both sides of the AI race: *"LET THEM RACE."*
- **The companies' founders and executives are not Lodge members** and don't know it exists. The Table owns pieces of what they build and nudges at the edges: a board seat filled by a family-office nominee, a funding round arriving at the right moment. That keeps the Lodge invisible and makes it more frightening.

### The families
- Every Seat holder's family is now very rich: houses on four continents, foundations, art, schools. Most relatives **don't know where the money's compass comes from**. They're told the family "has always been lucky with timing".
- Seats aren't hereditary (canon decision D1), but the Table prefers to seat people raised close to it. Several current Seats are children or in-laws of earlier holders.
- Secrecy is a family discipline: no photographs together, no shared events, different banks, different lawyers.

### The list (the real motive)
- The wealth was never the point. The Seats were promised that, at the end of the lease, **their families would be Taken** (see [the-tenants.md](the-tenants.md), "The lease").
- Every Factor, every Steward and every family office is working, unknowingly, to keep a few hundred names on that list.
- **The tragedy:** the Taken are simply the easiest to harvest. The Lodge has spent sixty years making sure its children are first.
- **The Lodge isn't the first Table** (see [deep time](deep-time.md)). Earlier circles were promised the same thing. Nobody knows what became of them.

### Running the campaign through subordinates
The Seats themselves almost never act. Orders pass down three layers, each knowing less:

| Layer | Who | Knows about the Tenants? |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats** | The twelve | Yes ("Weather ✓") |
| **Factors** | The heads of each family office: lawyers, bankers, fund managers who run the money and pass on instructions | No. They think they serve an eccentric, very successful family. |
| **Stewards** | Fixers, analysts, account operators, drivers (Cobb, Len's "cash man", the @longweather_ team) | No ("Weather ✗"). They do tasks without seeing the design. |

## Structure

| Role | Count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Founders** | 3 (now all dead) | Original contactees; wrote the Minutes |
| **Seats** | 12 (+1 empty) | Voting members; each numbered chair carries a fixed Portfolio and passes to the next person seated. Seat Thirteen is the Tenants' and is never sat in. |
| **Factors** | 12 offices | Run each Seat's family office and pass instructions down. Don't know about the Tenants. |
| **Stewards** | a few hundred worldwide | Unwitting or semi-witting helpers who carry out tasks without knowing the whole design |
| **The Listener** | 1 | The only member permitted to receive Tenant counsel directly; the role passes on death |

### Portfolios
Each Seat holds a broad area of slow influence: **Faith**, **Assembly** (organised labour), **Ledger** (money and prices), **Hearth** (family and housing), **Signal** (media and attention), **Engine** (technology), **Border** (movement of people), and others. The full seat-by-seat table is in [canon decisions D1](canon-decisions.md).

**Writing note:** the **Border** Portfolio's job in canon is to *manufacture conflict about* migration through panic, rumour and blame. Migrants themselves have no part in the plan. They are among its targets, like everyone else. The Lodge pushes every side of every quarrel at once, because the quarrel is the point.

## Methods

The Lodge almost never acts directly. Its tools are:

1. **Nudges.** Tiny, deniable changes: a funding priority, a delayed report, a rumour placed at the right dinner party.
2. **Pressure tides.** Small stresses timed to land together so that the strain compounds.
3. **Decoys.** Every crisis is paired with a convenient villain for the public to blame. *The Lodge wants you angry at your neighbour.*
4. **Silence.** No Seat has ever been photographed with another.

## Internal fractures (story engines)

- **The Accountants and the Gardeners.** The Accountants believe the Tenants' plan is benevolent stewardship. The Gardeners have begun to suspect that "pruning" means something they never agreed to.
- **The 1987 Walkout.** Seat Seven left the Table, then died in an unexplained "boating accident". Seat Seven's papers are the source of the Archivist's documents.
- **The succession problem.** The current Listener is old, and nobody knows who the Tenants will choose next.
- **Seat Eleven (Commons)** is the present-day Gardener: quietly recording doubts since Mother Quill's "let her be the one who explains it". In the book (ch 27) Seat Eleven quotes Marguerite and leaves, **the first Seat lost in thirty-nine years**.

## Symbols & tells (for ARG puzzles)

- **The empty thirteenth chair**, which appears in sketches and doodles in the documents. It is the Tenants' place, not a vacancy.
- **The phrase "for the long weather"**, used as a sign-off.
- **Harrowgate grey (#6B6E70)**, used on letterheads.
- **Dates written as day-count since 14 Oct 1962** (e.g. "Day 22,640").

## Hard-rule reminders

- No Seat is ever modelled on, named after, or hinted to be a real person.
- No real institution is described as a Lodge front. Invented fronts only: **the Harlow-Vane Institute for Systems Studies**, **Larkspire Mutual Re**, **the Ashby Trust**.
- **No real company is ever shown as Lodge-owned, Lodge-funded or Lodge-influenced.** The Table's holdings are in **fictional** global companies only (Pellucid, Tollgate, Halvard Dynamics, Nimfold, Lanternwork). Real companies such as Apple, Amazon, Tesla or any real AI lab must never be named or clearly imitated as Table investments.
